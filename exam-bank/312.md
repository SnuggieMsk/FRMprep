**Q1.** A trader writes 8 naked put contracts with strike $K = \$60$ when the stock is at $S = \$64$. The put premium is $2.50 per share. Under the standard exchange margin rules, the required margin is *closest* to:

- A. $8,000
- B. $8,400
- C. $10,000
- D. $13,200

**Answer: B**

We write **naked puts** and must compute the greater of the two standard margin formulas, then scale by shares and contracts. Note: the put is **out of the money** (stock $64 > strike $60), so the OTM amount reduces formula 1; for puts, formula 2 uses 10% of the **strike**.

1. Per-share **Formula 1**: premium + 20% of stock price − amount OTM. The put is OTM by $S - K = 64 - 60 = \$4$.
$$2.50 + 0.20(64) - 4 = 2.50 + 12.80 - 4.00 = 11.30$$
2. Per-share **Formula 2** (naked put → 10% of strike): $$2.50 + 0.10(60) = 2.50 + 6.00 = 8.50$$
3. Take the greater: $\max(11.30, 8.50) = 11.30$ per share.
4. Scale: $11.30 \times 100 \text{ shares} \times 8 \text{ contracts} = 11.30 \times 800 = \$9,040$.
