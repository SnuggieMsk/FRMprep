**Q1.** A discrete random variable $$X$$ takes the value $$-6$$ with probability 0.25, $$2$$ with probability 0.50, and $$10$$ with probability 0.25. What is the variance of $$X$$?

- A. 2.0
- B. 32.0
- C. 36.0
- D. 6.0

**Answer: B**

We need $$\mathrm{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$$.

1. Mean: $$E[X] = (-6)(0.25) + 2(0.50) + 10(0.25) = -1.5 + 1.0 + 2.5 = 2.0$$.
2. Second moment: $$E[X^2] = 36(0.25) + 4(0.50) + 100(0.25) = 9 + 2 + 25 = 36$$.
3. Variance: $$\mathrm{Var}(X) = 36 - (2.0)^2 = 36 - 4 = 32$$.

B is correct. A (2.0) is the mean, not the variance — answering the wrong statistic. C (36.0) is $$E[X^2]$$, forgetting to subtract $$(E[X])^2$$. D (6.0) is the standard deviation rounded ($$\sqrt{32}=5.66$$) confused with variance, or a partial sum.

📌 Exam tip: $$\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2$$. The most common slip is reporting $$E[X^2]$$ as the variance — always subtract the squared mean.

**Q2.** Daily P&L $$X$$ (in $ thousands) has mean 1.2, standard deviation 5, skewness $$-0.6$$, and kurtosis 4.5. The desk reports results as $$Y = -2X + 3$$. Which set of statistics for $$Y$$ is correct?

- A. Mean 0.6, std dev 10, skewness $$-0.6$$, kurtosis 4.5
- B. Mean 0.6, std dev 10, skewness $$+0.6$$, kurtosis 4.5
- C. Mean 0.6, std dev $$-10$$, skewness $$+0.6$$, kurtosis $$-4.5$$
- D. Mean $$-2.4$$, std dev 100, skewness $$+0.6$$, kurtosis 9.0

**Answer: B**

Apply linear-transformation rules to $$Y = aX + b$$ with $$a=-2$$, $$b=3$$.

1. Mean: $$a\mu + b = -2(1.2) + 3 = -2.4 + 3 = 0.6$$.
2. Standard deviation: $$|a|\sigma = |-2|\times 5 = 10$$ (always positive).
3. Skewness: sign flips because $$a<0$$, so $$-(-0.6) = +0.6$$.
4. Kurtosis: unchanged by any linear transform, so 4.5.

B is correct. A keeps the skewness sign $$-0.6$$, forgetting that a negative scale flips it. C reports a *negative* standard deviation (impossible — std dev is $$|a|\sigma$$) and negates kurtosis. D scales variance instead of std dev ($$a^2\sigma^2=4\times25=100$$ as "std dev"), forgets to add $$b$$ correctly to the mean, and doubles kurtosis.

📌 Exam tip: Under $$Y=aX+b$$: std dev uses $$|a|$$ (never negative), variance uses $$a^2$$, skewness sign flips iff $$a<0$$, and kurtosis is always unchanged. The shift $$b$$ affects only the mean.

**Q3.** A continuous random variable is uniformly distributed on the interval $$[3, 3.4]$$. Which statement is TRUE?

- A. Its PDF equals 1 everywhere on the interval, since probabilities cannot exceed 1
- B. Its PDF equals 2.5 on the interval, and $$P(X=3.2)=0$$
- C. Its PDF equals 0.4, and the probability that $$X$$ lies in $$[3, 3.4]$$ is 0.4
- D. $$P(X = 3.2) = 2.5$$

**Answer: B**

For a uniform on $$[a,b]$$, the density is $$f(x) = \frac{1}{b-a}$$, and point probabilities are zero.

1. Density: $$f(x) = \frac{1}{3.4 - 3} = \frac{1}{0.4} = 2.5$$ on the interval.
2. Note $$2.5 > 1$$ — perfectly valid, because density is not probability; only the *area* under the PDF must lie in $$[0,1]$$.
3. Total area: $$2.5 \times 0.4 = 1.0$$ (a valid distribution).
4. For any continuous variable, $$P(X = 3.2) = 0$$.

B is correct. A wrongly claims the PDF cannot exceed 1 — a density can be any nonnegative value. C confuses density with probability, calling $$f(x)=0.4$$ (it inverts the width) and claiming a probability that should be 1. D treats the density value 2.5 as a point probability — but for continuous variables $$P(X=x)=0$$.

📌 Exam tip: A PDF value can exceed 1; only areas (probabilities) are bounded by 1. And for any continuous variable $$P(X=\text{a single point})=0$$ — probability comes from intervals.

**Q4.** A bond's annual return $$X$$ (%) takes values $$-20$$ with probability 0.10, $$0$$ with probability 0.30, $$5$$ with probability 0.40, and $$15$$ with probability 0.20. What is the third central moment $$E[(X-\mu)^3]$$ (used in the skewness numerator)?

- A. 0
- B. $$-876.0$$
- C. $$+345.6$$
- D. $$-1{,}216.7$$

**Answer: B**

First the mean, then the third central moment.

1. Mean: $$E[X] = -20(0.10) + 0(0.30) + 5(0.40) + 15(0.20) = -2 + 0 + 2 + 3 = 3.0$$.
2. Deviations from mean: $$-23,\ -3,\ 2,\ 12$$.
3. Cubes of deviations: $$(-23)^3 = -12167$$; $$(-3)^3 = -27$$; $$(2)^3 = 8$$; $$(12)^3 = 1728$$.
4. Probability-weight:
$$E[(X-\mu)^3] = (-12167)(0.10) + (-27)(0.30) + (8)(0.40) + (1728)(0.20)$$
$$= -1216.7 - 8.1 + 3.2 + 345.6 = -876.0.$$

B is correct. A (0) wrongly assumes symmetry. C ($$+345.6$$) keeps only the positive $$+15\%$$ contribution and drops the dominant $$-20\%$$ term. D ($$-1216.7$$) is just the first term $$(-12167)(0.10)$$, forgetting to add the other three weighted cubes.

📌 Exam tip: Central moments use deviations *from the mean*, $$(X-\mu)$$, not raw $$X$$. The third moment's sign tells you the skew direction — here negative, a long left tail driven by the $$-20\%$$ outcome.

**Q5.** $$X$$ has mean 4 and variance 9. Define $$Y = X^2$$. Using $$E[X^2] = \mathrm{Var}(X) + (E[X])^2$$, what is $$E[Y]$$, and what does this illustrate about $$E[g(X)]$$ versus $$g(E[X])$$?

- A. $$E[Y] = 16$$; equal to $$(E[X])^2$$ because expectation is linear
- B. $$E[Y] = 25$$; greater than $$(E[X])^2 = 16$$, illustrating Jensen's inequality for a convex function
- C. $$E[Y] = 9$$; equal to the variance
- D. $$E[Y] = 25$$; less than $$(E[X])^2$$, illustrating Jensen's inequality

**Answer: B**

Compute $$E[Y]=E[X^2]$$ and compare to $$g(E[X])=(E[X])^2$$.

1. $$E[X^2] = \mathrm{Var}(X) + (E[X])^2 = 9 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$$.
2. $$g(E[X]) = (E[X])^2 = 16$$.
3. Since $$g(x)=x^2$$ is convex, Jensen's inequality gives $$E[g(X)] \ge g(E[X])$$: $$25 \ge 16$$. The gap (9) equals the variance.

B is correct. A applies linearity to a nonlinear function — wrong; $$E[X^2]\ne(E[X])^2$$ unless variance is zero. C reports the variance as $$E[Y]$$, confusing $$E[X^2]$$ with $$\mathrm{Var}(X)$$. D gets the value 25 right but inverts Jensen's direction: for a convex $$g$$, $$E[g(X)]$$ is *greater*, not less.

📌 Exam tip: Expectation is linear only for linear functions. For convex $$g$$ (like $$x^2$$), $$E[g(X)]\ge g(E[X])$$ by Jensen — the gap grows with variance.

**Q6.** A loss distribution has these quantiles: $$Q(0.25) = 2$$, $$Q(0.50) = 5$$, $$Q(0.75) = 11$$ ($ millions). A risk team rescales losses to a new currency at rate 1.5 and adds a fixed reserve of 4: $$Y = 1.5X + 4$$. What are the median and interquartile range (IQR) of $$Y$$?

- A. Median 11.5; IQR 13.5
- B. Median 11.5; IQR 9.0
- C. Median 7.5; IQR 9.0
- D. Median 11.5; IQR 17.5

**Answer: A**

Apply transformation rules to quantiles. For $$a>0$$: quantiles map as $$aQ+b$$; the IQR scales by $$|a|$$ and the additive constant $$b$$ cancels.

1. Median of $$Y$$ (a location measure, carries the +4): $$1.5 \times Q(0.50) + 4 = 1.5(5) + 4 = 7.5 + 4 = 11.5$$.
2. Original IQR: $$Q(0.75) - Q(0.25) = 11 - 2 = 9$$.
3. IQR of $$Y$$ (a spread measure, +4 cancels): $$|a| \times \text{IQR} = 1.5 \times 9 = 13.5$$.

A is correct: median 11.5, IQR 13.5. B keeps the IQR unscaled at 9, forgetting the multiplicative factor of 1.5 (the trap of treating IQR like a count). C drops the +4 from the median (reporting only $$1.5\times5=7.5$$) and leaves the IQR unscaled. D adds the +4 to the IQR as well ($$1.5\times9+4=17.5$$) — but additive constants never affect spread.

📌 Exam tip: Additive constants shift location measures (mean, median, quantiles) but NEVER affect spread measures (variance, std dev, IQR). Multiplicative factors scale both — by $$|a|$$ for spreads.

**Q7.** Monthly returns of a strategy have skewness $$-1.2$$ and excess kurtosis $$+5$$. An analyst describes the return distribution. Which description is most accurate?

- A. Symmetric with thin tails; large gains and large losses are equally rare and rarer than normal
- B. A long left tail (frequent small gains, occasional large losses) with fatter-than-normal tails overall (kurtosis 8)
- C. A long right tail with thin tails; kurtosis of 5
- D. Negative skew implies the mean exceeds the median, and excess kurtosis of 5 means kurtosis of 2

**Answer: B**

Interpret the moments.

1. Skewness $$-1.2 < 0$$: negatively skewed — a long *left* tail. Typical pattern: many small positive outcomes, rare large negative outcomes (the risk-management cocktail).
2. Excess kurtosis $$= \text{kurtosis} - 3$$. Given excess kurtosis $$+5$$, total kurtosis $$= 5 + 3 = 8 > 3$$ — leptokurtic, fatter tails than normal.

B is correct (long left tail, total kurtosis 8). A claims symmetry and thin tails — both contradicted by the signs. C says right tail (wrong sign) and thin tails (wrong). D mislabels: negative skew means the mean is typically *below* the median (left tail pulls the mean down), and "kurtosis of 2" wrongly subtracts 3 from excess kurtosis instead of adding it.

📌 Exam tip: Excess kurtosis = kurtosis − 3, so total kurtosis = excess + 3. Negative skew = long *left* tail, mean typically below median. Fat tails ⇔ kurtosis > 3 (excess > 0).
