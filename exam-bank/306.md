**Q1.** Four dealers clear interest rate swaps through a CCP. On a given day the gross bilateral obligations are: Dealer W owes X $80M, X owes Y $50M, Y owes Z $90M, and Z owes W $60M. After all positions are novated to the CCP and multilaterally netted, what is each dealer's net position versus the CCP, and what is the total of the net flows through the CCP?
- A. W pays $20M, X receives $30M, Y pays $40M, Z receives $30M; total net flows = $60M
- B. W receives $20M, X pays $30M, Y receives $40M, Z pays $30M; total net flows = $60M
- C. W pays $20M, X receives $30M, Y pays $40M, Z receives $30M; total net flows = $90M
- D. W pays $80M, X pays $50M, Y pays $90M, Z pays $60M; total net flows = $280M

**Answer: A**

The ask: compute each dealer's net position (receives − pays) against the CCP and the total net flows.

1. Tabulate each dealer's receives and pays (an "owes" obligation means that dealer *pays*):
   - W: pays 80 (to X), receives 60 (from Z) → net = 60 − 80 = −$20M → **W pays $20M**
   - X: pays 50 (to Y), receives 80 (from W) → net = 80 − 50 = +$30M → **X receives $30M**
   - Y: pays 90 (to Z), receives 50 (from X) → net = 50 − 90 = −$40M → **Y pays $40M**
   - Z: pays 60 (to W), receives 90 (from Y) → net = 90 − 60 = +$30M → **Z receives $30M**

2. Zero-sum check: −20 + 30 − 40 + 30 = 0. ✓ Payers are W ($20M) and Y ($40M); receivers are X ($30M) and Z ($30M).

3. Total net flows through the CCP = sum of one side = 20 + 40 = $60M (equivalently 30 + 30 = $60M).

This matches option **A**.

- **B** flips every direction (sign error: treating "owes" as "receives").
- **C** has the correct directions but reports $90M — a wrong residual (it forgot that the two payer amounts equal the two receiver amounts, $60M, not $90M).
- **D** ignores netting entirely and reports gross obligations ($280M), the un-netted bilateral total.

📌 Exam tip: Verify multilateral nets sum to zero, then total ONE side to get net flows. Gross bilateral flows here were 80+50+90+60 = $280M; multilateral netting compresses them to $60M.

**Q2.** A clearing member of a CCP defaults with a close-out loss of $620M. The defaulter had posted $250M of initial margin and $80M into the default fund. The CCP's pre-funded "skin in the game" is $40M. Surviving members' total default fund contributions are $900M. Which resources are consumed, in order, and how much of the surviving members' default fund is used?
- A. Defaulter's IM, then surviving members' IM, then CCP capital; $0 of survivors' default fund
- B. Defaulter's IM, defaulter's default fund, CCP skin in the game, then surviving members' default fund; $250M of survivors' default fund
- C. Defaulter's IM, defaulter's default fund, surviving members' default fund, then CCP skin in the game; $290M of survivors' default fund
- D. Defaulter's IM, CCP skin in the game, defaulter's default fund, then surviving members' default fund; $210M of survivors' default fund

**Answer: B**

The ask: apply the default waterfall in exact order and find how much of the survivors' mutualized default fund is drawn.

1. Waterfall order: (1) defaulter's IM → (2) defaulter's default fund → (3) CCP skin in the game → (4) surviving members' default fund → (5) assessments/recovery tools.

2. Absorb the $620M loss layer by layer:
   - Defaulter's IM: $250M absorbed; remaining loss = 620 − 250 = $370M.
   - Defaulter's default fund: $80M absorbed; remaining = 370 − 80 = $290M.
   - CCP skin in the game: $40M absorbed; remaining = 290 − 40 = $250M.
   - Surviving members' default fund: absorbs the final $250M (well within the $900M available).

3. Therefore $250M of survivors' default fund is used. The waterfall order is exactly: defaulter IM → defaulter default fund → CCP skin → survivors' default fund.

So $250M of the survivors' default fund is used, with the correct order: defaulter IM → defaulter default fund → CCP skin → survivors' default fund. This is option **B**.

- **A** is wrong because *surviving members' initial margin is never mutualized* — non-defaulters' IM is returned to them and cannot absorb another member's loss.
- **C** places survivors' default fund AHEAD of CCP skin in the game (wrong order: CCP capital comes first) and miscomputes the amount as $290M (it skipped the $40M CCP layer).
- **D** places CCP skin in the game ahead of the defaulter's own default fund — wrong, the defaulter's resources are always exhausted before the CCP contributes capital.

📌 Exam tip: The defaulter pays first and twice (IM then its default fund), THEN the CCP's own capital, THEN everyone else's default fund. Surviving members' INITIAL margin is sacrosanct — only their default-fund contributions and assessment rights are mutualized.

**Q3.** A risk manager is evaluating whether moving a portfolio of standardized OTC swaps from bilateral clearing into a CCP will reduce total margin posted. Currently, under a single ISDA master agreement, the firm nets a long interest-rate-swap book against a short book with the same counterparty, posting modest CSA collateral. The CCP would clear the interest-rate swaps but the firm's index CDS would go to a *different* CCP. Which effect is the most likely and is a recognized disadvantage of fragmented central clearing?
- A. Multilateral netting always reduces total margin, so margin must fall
- B. Splitting products across two CCPs can *destroy* netting benefits that existed bilaterally, potentially raising total margin
- C. Initial margin is never required at a CCP, so margin falls to zero
- D. The CCP eliminates variation margin, lowering daily funding needs

**Answer: B**

The ask: identify the recognized downside when previously-netted products are split across multiple CCPs.

1. Under one ISDA/CSA, all products with a counterparty can be netted bilaterally into a single exposure, reducing collateral.
2. A CCP nets multilaterally *within that CCP's product set*. If interest-rate swaps go to CCP A and index CDS go to CCP B, the two product books can no longer offset each other — netting that existed bilaterally is broken.
3. The firm now posts initial margin and variation margin separately at each CCP on gross-of-cross-product positions, which can *raise* total margin even though each CCP nets internally. This is the documented disadvantage: central clearing helps most when exposures are well distributed and concentrated in one venue; fragmentation reduces netting benefits.

- **A** overstates: multilateral netting helps *when not fragmented*; it can reduce benefits when products split across CCPs. The word "always" is the trap.
- **C** is false: CCP clearing *requires* initial margin (a key reason CCP margining is historically stricter than CSA collateral).
- **D** is false: VM is collected daily at CCPs, not eliminated.

📌 Exam tip: Central clearing is not unconditionally margin-reducing. When a single bilateral netting set is fragmented across multiple CCPs by product, the loss of cross-product offset can increase total collateral.

**Q4.** Which statement correctly distinguishes the principal-to-principal client clearing model from the agency (FCM) model?
- A. In the principal-to-principal model the client's contract is directly with the CCP; in the agency model the clearing member is principal to both legs
- B. In the principal-to-principal model the clearing member is principal to two back-to-back contracts (one with the client, one with the CCP); in the agency model the client faces the CCP and the member guarantees the client's performance
- C. Both models make the client face the CCP directly, differing only in margin segregation
- D. In the agency model the CCP bears no exposure to the client because no guarantee exists

**Answer: B**

The ask: correctly map each clearing model to who faces whom.

1. **Principal-to-principal (European model):** the client faces the clearing member; the member faces the CCP with a mirror (back-to-back) trade. The member is principal to *both* contracts.
2. **Agency (US FCM model):** the client's contract is *with the CCP*; the member acts as agent and *guarantees* the client's performance to the CCP.

B states exactly this.

- **A** swaps the two models (it describes agency as principal-to-principal and vice versa).
- **C** is wrong: only the agency model puts the client face-to-face with the CCP; principal-to-principal does not.
- **D** is wrong: in the agency model the member *guarantees* the client to the CCP — a guarantee is precisely what exists.

📌 Exam tip: "Principal-to-principal = member is principal to two contracts (back-to-back)." "Agency = client faces CCP, member guarantees." Both rely on client margin segregation (omnibus vs. individual) to make porting feasible on default.

**Q5.** Which of the following is correctly identified as a risk faced by the CCP itself (as opposed to a benefit of central clearing)?
- A. Multilateral netting reduces gross exposures across members
- B. Liquidity risk: the CCP must pay variation margin to winners on time even while the defaulter's collateral is still being liquidated
- C. Novation transfers counterparty risk away from members
- D. Transparency: the CCP observes all cleared positions

**Answer: B**

The ask: pick the genuine CCP risk among a list mixing risks and benefits.

1. A CCP runs a matched book, so it has no *market* risk in normal times, but it does bear: counterparty (default) risk, **liquidity risk**, investment risk on posted collateral, operational, and legal risk.
2. Liquidity risk specifically: when a member defaults, the CCP still owes variation margin to the winning members on schedule, but the defaulter's posted assets may take time to convert to cash. A timing mismatch between owed payments and asset liquidation is a classic CCP liquidity risk.

- **A**, **C**, and **D** are *advantages* of central clearing (netting, counterparty-risk reduction via novation, transparency), not risks borne by the CCP.

📌 Exam tip: Distinguish benefits (netting, transparency, counterparty-risk reduction) from CCP risks (default, liquidity, investment, operational, legal, model risk, procyclicality, concentration/"too big to fail").

**Q6.** A CCP's initial margin model targets a 99.5% confidence level over a 5-day margin period of risk. During a volatility spike, the model raises IM requirements sharply across all members. Which concept does this illustrate, and what is its systemic danger?
- A. Loss mutualization — survivors subsidize the defaulter
- B. Procyclicality — IM rises in stressed markets, draining member liquidity exactly when funding is scarce
- C. Novation — the CCP becomes counterparty to each trade
- D. Wrong-way risk — exposure rises as counterparty credit deteriorates

**Answer: B**

The ask: name the phenomenon where volatility-sensitive IM models amplify stress.

1. IM models (SPAN/VaR-type) scale with volatility and concentration. When volatility spikes, the modeled potential close-out loss rises, so required IM rises.
2. Higher IM demands more collateral from members precisely during stressed markets, when funding/liquidity is already scarce. This positive feedback between market stress and margin demands is **procyclicality** — a recognized systemic danger of CCP margining.

- **A** loss mutualization concerns absorbing a *defaulter's* losses via the default fund, not volatility-driven margin increases.
- **C** novation is the mechanics of the CCP interposing itself, unrelated to margin scaling.
- **D** wrong-way risk is exposure rising with counterparty credit deterioration — a related but distinct concept; here the trigger is *volatility*, not the counterparty's own credit.

📌 Exam tip: Procyclical margins = "more collateral demanded in stress." It drains liquidity from members when they can least afford it, potentially propagating the very stress the CCP is meant to contain.

**Q7.** Regarding loss mutualization and the resources available after a default, which statement is TRUE?
- A. A non-defaulting member's initial margin can be tapped to cover another member's default loss
- B. Variation margin gains haircutting and additional cash assessments on survivors are recovery tools used only after the default fund and CCP capital are exhausted
- C. The CCP's skin in the game is the very last resource consumed before resolution
- D. Surviving members' default fund contributions rank ahead of the CCP's own capital in the waterfall

**Answer: B**

The ask: identify the single correct statement about mutualization and the tail of the waterfall.

1. Recall the order: defaulter IM → defaulter default fund → CCP skin in the game → survivors' default fund → further assessments / recovery tools (rights of assessment, VM gains haircutting, partial tear-up) → resolution/failure.
2. VM gains haircutting and additional assessments sit in the *final* recovery layer, used only after the default fund(s) and CCP capital are exhausted. B is correct.

- **A** is false: non-defaulters' IM is never mutualized — it is returned to them.
- **C** is false: the CCP's skin in the game is consumed *before* survivors' default fund, not last; the last resources are assessments/recovery tools (then resolution).
- **D** is false: CCP capital ranks *ahead of* survivors' default fund (CCP "skin in the game" is consumed before survivors' mutualized contributions).

📌 Exam tip: Memorize the tail precisely — CCP skin in the game comes BEFORE survivors' default fund; assessments and VM gains haircutting are the *last* recovery tools before resolution. Non-defaulter IM is never touched.
