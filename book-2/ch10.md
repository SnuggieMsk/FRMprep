# 10. Stationary Time Series

For 2026, GARP shifted this chapter's LOs toward calculation: compute autocovariances and autocorrelations, derive AR/MA moments, find mean-reversion levels, and generate forecasts. Drill the numbers, not just the definitions.

## Learning objectives

- Describe the requirements for a series to be covariance-stationary.
- Define and apply the autocovariance and autocorrelation functions (ACF); define and calculate partial autocorrelations (PACF).
- Define white noise; describe independent white noise and normal (Gaussian) white noise.
- Define and describe the properties of autoregressive (AR) processes; explain mean reversion and calculate the mean-reverting level.
- Define and describe the properties of moving average (MA) processes.
- Explain how a lag operator works.
- Explain and apply the Box–Pierce and Ljung–Box Q-statistics.
- Describe the properties of ARMA processes and how forecasts are generated from ARMA models.
- Describe sample autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation, and how AR, MA, and ARMA models are identified.
- Explain how seasonality is modeled in a covariance-stationary ARMA framework.

## Covariance stationarity

A series $$\{Y_t\}$$ is **covariance-stationary** if three things are constant over time:

1. $$E[Y_t] = \mu$$ for all $$t$$;
2. $$\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = \gamma_0 < \infty$$ for all $$t$$;
3. $$\mathrm{Cov}(Y_t, Y_{t-h}) = \gamma_h$$ depends only on the lag $$h$$, not on $$t$$.

**Autocovariance function:** $$\gamma_h = \mathrm{Cov}(Y_t, Y_{t-h})$$. **Autocorrelation function (ACF):**

$$\rho_h = \frac{\gamma_h}{\gamma_0}, \qquad \rho_0 = 1, \quad \rho_h = \rho_{-h}$$

The **partial autocorrelation (PACF)** at lag $$h$$ is the correlation between $$Y_t$$ and $$Y_{t-h}$$ after controlling for the intermediate lags — operationally, the coefficient on $$Y_{t-h}$$ in an AR($$h$$) regression.

## White noise

$$\{\epsilon_t\}$$ is **white noise** if $$E[\epsilon_t] = 0$$, $$\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon_t) = \sigma^2$$, and $$\rho_h = 0$$ for all $$h \ne 0$$ (uncorrelated, not necessarily independent).

- **Independent white noise:** additionally iid.
- **Gaussian white noise:** iid $$N(0, \sigma^2)$$ — independence and normality.

White noise is unforecastable from its own past — it is the benchmark for model residuals: a well-specified model leaves white-noise residuals.

## Lag operators

The lag operator shifts time: $$L Y_t = Y_{t-1}$$, $$L^k Y_t = Y_{t-k}$$. An AR(1) $$Y_t = \delta + \phi Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$ becomes $$(1 - \phi L)Y_t = \delta + \epsilon_t$$. Lag polynomials let you express stationarity conditions compactly: an AR model is stationary when all roots of its lag polynomial lie outside the unit circle (for AR(1): $$\lvert \phi \rvert < 1$$).

## AR processes

**AR(1):**

$$Y_t = \delta + \phi Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t, \qquad \lvert\phi\rvert < 1 \text{ for stationarity}$$

Moments:

$$E[Y_t] = \frac{\delta}{1 - \phi}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = \frac{\sigma^2}{1 - \phi^2}, \qquad \rho_h = \phi^h$$

The **mean-reversion level** is the long-run mean $$\frac{\delta}{1-\phi}$$: when $$Y_t$$ is above it, the next move is expected downward, and vice versa. The ACF decays geometrically (slowly for $$\phi$$ near 1); the PACF **cuts off** after lag 1 (after lag $$p$$ for AR($$p$$)).

## MA processes

**MA(1):**

$$Y_t = \mu + \theta \epsilon_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$

Moments:

$$E[Y_t] = \mu, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = \sigma^2(1 + \theta^2), \qquad \rho_1 = \frac{\theta}{1 + \theta^2}, \quad \rho_h = 0 \text{ for } h \ge 2$$

MA processes are stationary for **any** $$\theta$$ (finite sums of white noise). The ACF **cuts off** after lag $$q$$ for MA($$q$$); the PACF decays gradually. Memory is finite: shocks last exactly $$q$$ periods.

## ARMA and model identification

**ARMA(1,1):** $$Y_t = \delta + \phi Y_{t-1} + \theta\epsilon_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$ — combines geometric ACF decay (from the AR part) with richer short-lag behavior. Mean is still $$\frac{\delta}{1-\phi}$$; stationarity depends only on the AR part. Neither ACF nor PACF cuts off — both decay.

| | ACF | PACF |
|---|---|---|
| AR($$p$$) | decays gradually (geometric/oscillating) | **cuts off after lag $$p$$** |
| MA($$q$$) | **cuts off after lag $$q$$** | decays gradually |
| ARMA($$p,q$$) | decays gradually | decays gradually |

**Forecasts** from an AR(1) iterate the recursion with future shocks set to zero:

$$\hat Y_{t+1} = \delta + \phi Y_t, \qquad \hat Y_{t+h} = \frac{\delta}{1-\phi} + \phi^h\left(Y_t - \frac{\delta}{1-\phi}\right)$$

Forecasts converge geometrically to the mean-reversion level as $$h$$ grows. For an MA($$q$$), forecasts beyond $$q$$ steps equal $$\mu$$ exactly.

## Testing for serial correlation: Q-statistics

**Box–Pierce:**

$$Q_{BP} = T\sum_{h=1}^{m} \hat\rho_h^2$$

**Ljung–Box** (better in small samples):

$$Q_{LB} = T(T+2)\sum_{h=1}^{m} \frac{\hat\rho_h^2}{T - h}$$

Under $$H_0$$ (white noise), both are $$\sim \chi^2_m$$. Reject for large values ⇒ the series (or residuals) are serially correlated. Sample ACF standard error under white noise: $$1/\sqrt{T}$$, so individual $$\hat\rho_h$$ outside $$\pm 1.96/\sqrt{T}$$ are significant.

## Seasonality

Deterministic seasonality: add seasonal dummy variables. Stochastic seasonality: include seasonal lags, e.g. a seasonal AR for monthly data uses $$Y_{t-12}$$. Seasonal differencing ($$Y_t - Y_{t-12}$$) removes repeating annual patterns while preserving covariance stationarity of the remainder.

{: .warning }
Mean-reversion trap: the level is $$\frac{\delta}{1-\phi}$$, **not** $$\delta$$. With $$\phi$$ close to 1, a small intercept implies a large long-run mean (e.g., $$\delta = 0.2$$, $$\phi = 0.95$$ ⇒ mean 4.0). Also check stationarity first: if $$\phi \ge 1$$ there is no mean reversion at all — the formula is meaningless.

### Worked example: AR(1) moments and mean reversion

An interest-rate model: $$r_t = 0.6 + 0.85\,r_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$, $$\sigma_\epsilon = 0.4$$ (% points). The current rate is $$r_t = 5.0\%$$.

**Mean-reversion level:**

$$\mu = \frac{0.6}{1 - 0.85} = \frac{0.6}{0.15} = 4.0\%$$

**Unconditional variance and ACF:**

$$\mathrm{Var}(r) = \frac{0.4^2}{1 - 0.85^2} = \frac{0.16}{0.2775} = 0.5766, \qquad \rho_1 = 0.85, \quad \rho_2 = 0.7225$$

**Forecasts:**

$$\hat r_{t+1} = 0.6 + 0.85 \times 5.0 = 4.85\%$$

$$\hat r_{t+2} = 0.6 + 0.85 \times 4.85 = 4.7225\% \quad \left(= 4.0 + 0.85^2 \times 1.0\right)$$

The rate is above its long-run mean, so forecasts glide down toward 4.0%.

### Worked example: MA(1) moments

$$Y_t = 2 + 0.5\,\epsilon_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$ with $$\sigma_\epsilon^2 = 4$$.

$$E[Y] = 2, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(Y) = 4(1 + 0.25) = 5$$

$$\rho_1 = \frac{0.5}{1 + 0.25} = 0.40, \qquad \rho_2 = \rho_3 = \cdots = 0$$

One-step forecast given $$\epsilon_t = 1.2$$: $$\hat Y_{t+1} = 2 + 0.5 \times 1.2 = 2.6$$. Two steps ahead: $$\hat Y_{t+2} = 2$$ (the mean — MA(1) memory is one period).

### Worked example: Ljung–Box test

Residuals from a model, $$T = 100$$, first three sample autocorrelations: $$\hat\rho_1 = 0.20$$, $$\hat\rho_2 = 0.10$$, $$\hat\rho_3 = 0.05$$. Test white noise with $$m = 3$$ at 5%.

$$Q_{LB} = 100 \times 102 \times \left(\frac{0.04}{99} + \frac{0.01}{98} + \frac{0.0025}{97}\right)$$

$$= 10200 \times (0.000404 + 0.000102 + 0.0000258) = 10200 \times 0.000532 = 5.42$$

Critical $$\chi^2_3$$ at 5% = 7.81. Since $$5.42 < 7.81$$, fail to reject — residuals are consistent with white noise. (Note $$\hat\rho_1 = 0.20$$ alone is borderline: $$1.96/\sqrt{100} = 0.196$$.)

## Quick quiz

**1.** An AR(1) has $$\delta = 1.5$$ and $$\phi = 0.7$$. Its mean-reverting level is:

- A. 1.5
- B. 2.14
- C. 5.0
- D. 0.45

**2.** For an MA(2) process, the ACF:

- A. Decays geometrically forever
- B. Is zero at all lags
- C. Cuts off to zero after lag 2
- D. Equals the PACF at every lag

**3.** Which is required for covariance stationarity?

- A. The series is normally distributed
- B. Autocovariances depend only on the lag, with constant finite mean and variance
- C. The series has zero mean
- D. The series is independent over time

**4.** AR(1) with $$\phi = 0.6$$, mean 10, current value 16. The 2-step-ahead forecast is:

- A. 12.16
- B. 13.60
- C. 10.00
- D. 16.00

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1. C** — $$1.5/(1 - 0.7) = 5.0$$. (B is the bait $$1.5/0.7$$.)

**2. C** — MA($$q$$) ACF cuts off after lag $$q$$; the PACF is the one that decays.

**3. B** — The three stationarity conditions; normality and independence are not required.

**4. A** — $$\hat Y_{t+2} = 10 + 0.6^2(16 - 10) = 10 + 0.36 \times 6 = 12.16$$.
