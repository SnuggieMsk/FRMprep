# 2. Random Variables

This chapter builds the machinery used everywhere else in Book 2: PMF/PDF/CDF, expectation and variance algebra, the four moments, and quantiles. Expect direct calculations of $$E[X]$$, $$\mathrm{Var}(X)$$, and transformed variables $$aX+b$$.

## Learning objectives

- Describe and distinguish a probability mass function (PMF), a cumulative distribution function (CDF), and a probability density function (PDF).
- Understand and apply the concept of a mathematical expectation of a random variable.
- Describe the four common population moments: mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis.
- Explain the differences between a probability mass function and a probability density function.
- Characterize the quantile function and quantile-based estimators.
- Explain the effect of a linear transformation of a random variable on the mean, variance, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, median, and interquartile range.

## PMF, PDF, and CDF

| Concept | Discrete RV | Continuous RV |
|---|---|---|
| Probability of a point | PMF: $$f(x) = P(X = x)$$ | $$P(X = x) = 0$$; PDF $$f(x)$$ gives density, not probability |
| Probability of a range | Sum the PMF | Integrate the PDF: $$P(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$ |
| Cumulative | $$F(x) = \sum_{t \le x} f(t)$$ | $$F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t)\,dt$$ |

The CDF $$F(x) = P(X \le x)$$ is nondecreasing, runs from 0 to 1, and works identically for both types. For continuous variables, $$f(x) = F'(x)$$.

{: .warning }
A PDF value can exceed 1 (e.g., a uniform on $$[0, 0.5]$$ has $$f(x) = 2$$). Only *probabilities* — areas under the PDF — must lie in $$[0,1]$$. Distractors that reject $$f(x) > 1$$ as "impossible" are wrong.

## Expectation

For discrete $$X$$: $$E[X] = \sum_i x_i\,p_i$$. For continuous $$X$$: $$E[X] = \int x f(x)\,dx$$.

Expectation is **linear**:

$$E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b, \qquad E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y]$$

Linearity of $$E[X+Y]$$ holds **always**, even when $$X$$ and $$Y$$ are dependent. But in general $$E[g(X)] \ne g(E[X])$$ for nonlinear $$g$$ (Jensen's inequality: for convex $$g$$, $$E[g(X)] \ge g(E[X])$$).

## The four population moments

**Variance** (second central moment):

$$\sigma^2 = \mathrm{Var}(X) = E[(X-\mu)^2] = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$$

**Skewness** (standardized third central moment) — measures asymmetry:

$$\text{skew} = \frac{E[(X-\mu)^3]}{\sigma^3}$$

**Kurtosis** (standardized fourth central moment) — measures tail heaviness:

$$\text{kurt} = \frac{E[(X-\mu)^4]}{\sigma^4}$$

Normal kurtosis is 3; **excess kurtosis** = kurt − 3. Kurtosis above 3 ("leptokurtic," fat tails) is the empirical norm for financial returns. Negative skew + excess kurtosis = the classic risk-management cocktail: frequent small gains, rare large losses.

## Linear transformations: $$Y = aX + b$$

| Statistic of $$Y = aX + b$$ | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean | $$a\mu + b$$ |
| Variance | $$a^2 \sigma^2$$ |
| Standard deviation | $$\lvert a \rvert \sigma$$ |
| Skewness | unchanged if $$a>0$$; sign flips if $$a<0$$ |
| Kurtosis | unchanged |
| Median / quantiles | $$a \cdot \text{median} + b$$ (for $$a > 0$$) |
| IQR | $$\lvert a \rvert \cdot \text{IQR}$$ |

{: .warning }
Variance scales by $$a^2$$, not $$a$$ — and the additive constant $$b$$ shifts the mean but does **nothing** to variance, skewness, or kurtosis. Both facts are recycled endlessly as distractors.

## Quantiles

The quantile function inverts the CDF: $$Q(\alpha) = F^{-1}(\alpha)$$ is the value below which the variable falls with probability $$\alpha$$. The median is $$Q(0.5)$$; the interquartile range is

$$\text{IQR} = Q(0.75) - Q(0.25)$$

Quantile-based measures (median, IQR) are robust to outliers and exist even when moments do not (e.g., Cauchy). VaR is precisely a quantile of the loss distribution, which is why this machinery matters for FRM.

### Worked example: moments of a discrete payoff

A trade pays $$-10$$ with probability 0.2, $$0$$ with probability 0.5, and $$+8$$ with probability 0.3.

**Mean:**

$$E[X] = (-10)(0.2) + 0(0.5) + 8(0.3) = -2.0 + 0 + 2.4 = 0.4$$

**Second moment and variance:**

$$E[X^2] = 100(0.2) + 0 + 64(0.3) = 20 + 19.2 = 39.2$$

$$\sigma^2 = 39.2 - 0.4^2 = 39.04, \qquad \sigma = 6.248$$

**Skewness:** third central moment

$$E[(X-0.4)^3] = (-10.4)^3(0.2) + (-0.4)^3(0.5) + (7.6)^3(0.3)$$

$$= (-1124.864)(0.2) + (-0.064)(0.5) + (438.976)(0.3) = -224.97 - 0.03 + 131.69 = -93.31$$

$$\text{skew} = \frac{-93.31}{6.248^3} = \frac{-93.31}{243.9} = -0.383$$

Negatively skewed: the rare $$-10$$ loss drags the tail left even though the mean is positive.

### Worked example: linear transformation

Daily P&L $$X$$ has mean 0.5 and standard deviation 4 (in $ thousands). The desk converts to euros at a fixed rate of 0.9 and subtracts a fixed cost of 0.2: $$Y = 0.9X - 0.2$$.

$$E[Y] = 0.9(0.5) - 0.2 = 0.25$$

$$\sigma_Y = 0.9 \times 4 = 3.6, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(Y) = 0.81 \times 16 = 12.96$$

Skewness and kurtosis of $$Y$$ equal those of $$X$$ (positive scale, shift irrelevant).

## Quick quiz

**1.** $$X$$ has $$E[X] = 2$$ and $$E[X^2] = 13$$. The standard deviation of $$X$$ is:

- A. 9
- B. 3
- C. 11
- D. 3.61

**2.** If $$Y = -2X + 5$$ and $$X$$ has skewness $$+0.8$$ and kurtosis 5, then $$Y$$ has:

- A. Skewness $$+0.8$$, kurtosis 5
- B. Skewness $$-0.8$$, kurtosis 5
- C. Skewness $$-0.8$$, kurtosis $$-5$$
- D. Skewness $$+0.8$$, kurtosis 10

**3.** Which statement about a continuous random variable is TRUE?

- A. Its PDF must never exceed 1
- B. $$P(X = x) = 0$$ for every point $$x$$
- C. Its CDF can decrease over some intervals
- D. The PDF gives $$P(X \le x)$$

**4.** Returns exhibit kurtosis of 6. Relative to a normal distribution with the same variance, this distribution has:

- A. Thinner tails and excess kurtosis of 6
- B. Fatter tails and excess kurtosis of 3
- C. Fatter tails and excess kurtosis of 6
- D. The same tails; kurtosis only measures asymmetry

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1. B** — $$\sigma^2 = 13 - 2^2 = 9$$, so $$\sigma = 3$$.

**2. B** — A negative scale flips skewness sign; kurtosis is unaffected by any linear transformation.

**3. B** — Point probabilities are zero for continuous variables; densities can exceed 1; CDFs are nondecreasing.

**4. B** — Excess kurtosis $$= 6 - 3 = 3 > 0$$ means fatter-than-normal tails (leptokurtic).

</details>
