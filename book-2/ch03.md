# 3. Common Univariate Random Variables

GARP expects you to know the catalog: when each distribution applies, its mean and variance, and how the testing distributions (chi-square, t, F) are built from normals. Binomial, Poisson, and normal probability calculations are routine exam questions.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish key properties and identify common occurrences of the following: uniform, Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, normal, lognormal, chi-squared, Student's t, and F-distributions.
- Describe a mixture distribution and explain the creation and characteristics of mixture distributions.

## Master table

| Distribution | Parameters | Mean | Variance | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bernoulli | $$p$$ | $$p$$ | $$p(1-p)$$ | Single default / no default |
| Binomial | $$n, p$$ | $$np$$ | $$np(1-p)$$ | Defaults in a portfolio of $$n$$ independent credits |
| Poisson | $$\lambda$$ | $$\lambda$$ | $$\lambda$$ | Operational-loss event counts per period |
| Continuous uniform | $$a, b$$ | $$\frac{a+b}{2}$$ | $$\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$$ | Random number generation, priors |
| Normal | $$\mu, \sigma^2$$ | $$\mu$$ | $$\sigma^2$$ | Returns (approximation), test statistics |
| Lognormal | $$\mu, \sigma^2$$ of $$\ln X$$ | $$e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2}$$ | $$(e^{\sigma^2}-1)e^{2\mu+\sigma^2}$$ | Asset *prices* (always positive) |
| Chi-squared | $$k$$ d.o.f. | $$k$$ | $$2k$$ | Variance tests; sum of $$k$$ squared standard normals |
| Student's t | $$\nu$$ d.o.f. | $$0$$ ($$\nu>1$$) | $$\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}$$ ($$\nu>2$$) | Means with unknown variance; fat-tailed returns |
| F | $$\nu_1, \nu_2$$ | $$\frac{\nu_2}{\nu_2-2}$$ ($$\nu_2>2$$) | — | Ratio of variances; regression overall fit |

## Discrete distributions

**Bernoulli($$p$$):** $$X \in \{0, 1\}$$ with $$P(X=1)=p$$. Building block for default modeling.

**Binomial($$n, p$$):** number of successes in $$n$$ independent Bernoulli trials:

$$P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$$

**Poisson($$\lambda$$):** count of events in an interval when events arrive independently at constant rate:

$$P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$

Mean = variance = $$\lambda$$ — a signature fact. Poisson approximates binomial when $$n$$ is large and $$p$$ small ($$\lambda = np$$). Poisson counts over disjoint periods add: events per month $$\lambda$$ implies events per year $$12\lambda$$.

## Continuous distributions

**Uniform($$a,b$$):** $$f(x) = \frac{1}{b-a}$$ on $$[a,b]$$.

**Normal($$\mu, \sigma^2$$):** symmetric, skew 0, kurtosis 3, fully described by two parameters; any linear combination of jointly normal variables is normal. Standardize with

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Memorize: 68% of mass within $$\pm 1\sigma$$, 90% within $$\pm 1.645\sigma$$, 95% within $$\pm 1.96\sigma$$, 99% within $$\pm 2.576\sigma$$. One-tailed: 5% beyond 1.645, 1% beyond 2.326.

**Lognormal:** $$X$$ is lognormal if $$\ln X$$ is normal. Bounded below by zero and right-skewed — the standard model for prices when log returns are normal.

**Chi-squared($$k$$):** $$\sum_{i=1}^k Z_i^2$$ for independent standard normals. Right-skewed, positive support; skew shrinks as $$k$$ grows. Used to test a single variance: $$(n-1)s^2/\sigma_0^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-1}$$.

**Student's t($$\nu$$):** $$t = \frac{Z}{\sqrt{W/\nu}}$$ with $$Z$$ standard normal, $$W \sim \chi^2_\nu$$. Symmetric, fatter tails than normal; converges to the normal as $$\nu \to \infty$$. Variance $$\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}$$ exceeds 1.

**F($$\nu_1, \nu_2$$):** ratio of two independent chi-squareds each divided by its d.o.f. Right-skewed, positive. Note $$t_\nu^2 = F_{1,\nu}$$.

{: .warning }
Returns ≈ normal, prices ≈ lognormal. If the question says "log returns are normally distributed," the *price* is lognormal and can never go below zero. Mixing these up is the chapter's classic trap. Second trap: the t-distribution has *fatter* tails than the normal, so t critical values are *larger* — confidence intervals widen, they don't shrink.

## Mixture distributions

A mixture draws from one of several component distributions, chosen at random with mixing weights $$w_i$$ ($$\sum w_i = 1$$). Its PDF is the weighted average of component PDFs:

$$f(x) = \sum_i w_i f_i(x)$$

The mixture's mean is the weighted mean of component means. Even a mixture of two normals with equal means but different variances produces **excess kurtosis** (fat tails); different means can produce skewness or bimodality. Mixtures are a simple way to model regimes — e.g., a "calm" normal 95% of the time and a "crisis" normal 5% of the time.

{: .note }
The variance of a mixture is *not* simply the weighted average of variances unless all component means are equal: $$\sigma^2 = \sum_i w_i(\sigma_i^2 + \mu_i^2) - \mu^2$$.

### Worked example: binomial default count

A portfolio holds 10 independent loans, each with default probability 4%. Probability of exactly 2 defaults in a year:

$$P(X=2) = \binom{10}{2}(0.04)^2(0.96)^8 = 45 \times 0.0016 \times 0.7214 = 0.0519 \approx 5.2\%$$

Mean defaults: $$np = 0.4$$. Variance: $$np(1-p) = 10 \times 0.04 \times 0.96 = 0.384$$.

Probability of at least one default: $$1 - 0.96^{10} = 1 - 0.6648 = 0.3352$$.

### Worked example: Poisson operational losses

System outages follow a Poisson process averaging $$\lambda = 2$$ per quarter.

$$P(X = 0) = e^{-2} = 0.1353, \qquad P(X = 3) = \frac{2^3 e^{-2}}{3!} = \frac{8 \times 0.1353}{6} = 0.1804$$

$$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - P(0) - P(1) = 1 - 0.1353 - 2(0.1353) = 1 - 0.4060 = 0.5940$$

Annual outages are Poisson with $$\lambda = 8$$, so mean 8 and variance 8.

### Worked example: normal probability

Monthly returns are $$N(1\%, 5\%^2)$$. Probability of a return below $$-7.25\%$$:

$$z = \frac{-7.25 - 1}{5} = -1.65 \;\Rightarrow\; P \approx 0.05 = 5\%$$

The return $$-7.25\%$$ is the 5% quantile — i.e., the monthly 95% VaR is 7.25% of portfolio value.

## Quick quiz

**1.** Operational events arrive at a Poisson rate of 1.5 per month. The probability of exactly 2 events next month is closest to:

- A. 22.3%
- B. 25.1%
- C. 33.5%
- D. 18.7%

**2.** Which distribution is bounded below by zero and right-skewed, and is the standard model for asset prices?

- A. Normal
- B. Student's t
- C. Lognormal
- D. Uniform

**3.** The sum of the squares of 12 independent standard normal variables follows:

- A. $$N(0, 12)$$
- B. $$\chi^2_{12}$$ with mean 12 and variance 24
- C. $$t_{12}$$
- D. $$F_{12,12}$$

**4.** A 50/50 mixture of $$N(0, 1)$$ and $$N(0, 9)$$ has, relative to a single normal with the same overall variance:

- A. Negative skew
- B. Excess kurtosis (fatter tails)
- C. Kurtosis below 3
- D. Identical shape

**2. C** — Lognormal: positive support, right skew, normal log returns.

**3. B** — Definition of chi-squared with $$k=12$$; mean $$k = 12$$, variance $$2k = 24$$.

**4. B** — Variance mixtures with common mean are symmetric but leptokurtic — extra mass in the center and tails.
