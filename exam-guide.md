# Exam Guide

Everything logistical and tactical about sitting FRM Part I.

## Registration and logistics

- Register at [garp.org/frm](https://www.garp.org/frm). First-time candidates
  pay a one-time enrollment fee plus the exam fee; early registration is
  meaningfully cheaper than standard. Check current fees on the site.
- Part I is offered in **May, August, and November** windows at Pearson VUE /
  PSI computer-based testing centers. Book your seat early — popular centers
  fill up.
- You have a multi-year window to pass Part II after passing Part I, and you
  must subsequently demonstrate two years of relevant work experience to be
  certified. Details: GARP candidate guide (see [Resources](resources)).

## The format, and what it implies

- **100 MCQs, 4 hours** → 2.4 minutes per question average. Many definition
  questions take 30 seconds; bank that time for the multi-step calculations.
- **Four answer choices**, exactly one correct. Distractors are engineered
  from common errors: sign flips, using the wrong compounding convention,
  forgetting to annualize, confusing duration with modified duration.
- **No negative marking.** Answer everything. With one minute left, fill in
  every blank — an unanswered question is a guaranteed zero.
- **No formula sheet is provided.** Everything on the
  [Formula Sheets](formula-sheets/) must live in your head.

## Calculator mastery (TI BA II Plus)

The TI BA II Plus is the most popular choice. Whatever you use, these
operations must be automatic before exam day:

| Skill | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| TVM keys (N, I/Y, PV, PMT, FV) | Bond pricing, annuities — used constantly |
| `2ND` → `P/Y` set to 1 (and *leave it*) | The #1 source of silent wrong answers |
| `2ND` → `CLR TVM` before every TVM problem | Stale register values are lethal |
| Cash-flow worksheet (CF, NPV, IRR) | Irregular cash flows |
| `ICONV` (interest conversion) | Nominal ↔ effective rate switches |
| `y^x`, `LN`, `e^x` | Continuous compounding appears everywhere in Part I |
| STAT worksheet (1-V, LIN) | Means, standard deviations, regression slope |
| Memory stores (STO/RCL 0–9) | Chain calculations without rounding error |

{: .warning }
Set decimal display to at least 4 places (`2ND` → `FORMAT` → `DEC = 4` or
`9`). Two-decimal rounding mid-calculation will push you onto wrong answer
choices that were designed to catch exactly that.

## Question-answering strategy

1. **Three passes.** Pass 1: answer everything that takes <2 minutes; flag the
   rest. Pass 2: the flagged calculations. Pass 3: sanity-check flags and fill
   any blanks.
2. **Read the last sentence first** on long stems — it tells you what is
   actually being asked, so you know what to extract from the story.
3. **Watch units and conventions**: annual vs. semiannual, continuous vs.
   discrete compounding, price vs. rate volatility, one-tailed vs. two-tailed.
4. **Estimate before computing.** If a bond trades below par and coupons are
   8%, yield must exceed 8% — kill impossible choices before doing math.
5. **A "too easy" answer in a multi-step problem is usually the trap** — it's
   the value you get if you stop one step early. Distractors are built that way.

## Exam-day checklist

- Government-issued passport-grade ID matching your registration name exactly.
- Approved calculator (bring a spare or fresh batteries) — only the models on
  GARP's list are allowed; the proctor may inspect or clear memory.
- Arrive 30+ minutes early; budget for security check-in.
- No scratch paper of your own — the center provides erasable boards/sheets.
- Plan your time: at the 2-hour mark you should be at or past question 50.

## The night before

Nothing new. Formula sheet skim, calculator battery check, route to the test
center, sleep. A rested candidate scoring 70% beats an exhausted one who
crammed to 75% — execution errors under fatigue are the real killer.
