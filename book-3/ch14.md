# 14. Trading Strategies

GARP tests strategy construction (which options, which strikes, long or short), the resulting payoff/profit diagrams, and the numbers: max profit, max loss, and breakevens. Know which strategies bet on direction and which bet on volatility.

## Learning objectives

- Explain the motivation for, and payoff of, a covered call and a protective put, and relate them to put-call parity.
- Describe principal-protected notes and the conditions needed to create them.
- Describe the construction, uses, and payoff functions of spread strategies: bull, bear, box, butterfly, calendar, and diagonal spreads.
- Describe the construction, uses, and payoff functions of combination strategies: straddles, strangles, strips, straps, and collars.
- Compute payoff, profit, maximum profit, maximum loss, and breakeven for each strategy.

## Option plus stock

- **Covered call**: long stock + short call. Earns the premium; gives up upside above $$K$$. By put-call parity, $$S_0 - c = Ke^{-rT} - p$$, so the profit profile looks like a **short put**.
- **Protective put**: long stock + long put. Insurance against a fall; profile looks like a **long call** (again via parity: $$S_0 + p = c + Ke^{-rT}$$).

The parity link is the exam point: stock plus one option always replicates the *opposite* position in the other option, plus cash.

## Principal-protected notes

A PPN packages a **zero-coupon bond plus a call option** so the investor gets the principal back at maturity plus equity upside. With principal of $100, 3 years, and $$r = 4\%$$ continuous, the zero costs $$100e^{-0.12} = 100(0.8869) = \$88.69$$, leaving $11.31 to buy the call. PPNs are **hard to create when interest rates are low** (the zero absorbs nearly all of the $100, leaving little for the option) **or when volatility is high** (the call is expensive). Issuers cheapen the embedded option via higher strikes, caps, averaging, or knock-outs.

## Spreads (same type of option)

Let $$K_1 < K_2 < K_3$$. "Debit" = pay net premium up front; "credit" = receive it.

| Spread | Construction | Debit/credit | Max profit | Max loss | Breakeven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull call | Buy call $$K_1$$, sell call $$K_2$$ | Debit $$D$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ | $$D$$ | $$K_1 + D$$ |
| Bull put | Buy put $$K_1$$, sell put $$K_2$$ | Credit $$C$$ | $$C$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - C$$ | $$K_2 - C$$ |
| Bear call | Sell call $$K_1$$, buy call $$K_2$$ | Credit $$C$$ | $$C$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - C$$ | $$K_1 + C$$ |
| Bear put | Buy put $$K_2$$, sell put $$K_1$$ | Debit $$D$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ | $$D$$ | $$K_2 - D$$ |
| Long call butterfly | Buy calls $$K_1, K_3$$; sell 2 calls $$K_2$$ | Debit $$D$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ at $$S_T = K_2$$ | $$D$$ | $$K_1 + D$$ and $$K_3 - D$$ |

Generic bull call spread payoff:

| Range | Long $$K_1$$ call | Short $$K_2$$ call | Total payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| $$S_T \le K_1$$ | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $$K_1 < S_T < K_2$$ | $$S_T - K_1$$ | 0 | $$S_T - K_1$$ |
| $$S_T \ge K_2$$ | $$S_T - K_1$$ | $$-(S_T - K_2)$$ | $$K_2 - K_1$$ |

- **Box spread** = bull call spread + bear put spread on the same $$K_1, K_2$$. Payoff is **always $$K_2 - K_1$$**, so its fair value is the PV, $$(K_2 - K_1)e^{-rT}$$. Any other price is an arbitrage — but only with **European** options (American early exercise breaks the box).
- **Butterfly**: bets that $$S_T$$ stays **near $$K_2$$** (short volatility) with small, capped risk either way.
- **Calendar spread**: sell a short-dated call, buy a long-dated call, **same strike**. Profits if $$S_T$$ is close to $$K$$ at the *short* maturity (the short option dies worthless while the long option keeps time value). Loses if the stock moves far either way.
- **Diagonal spread**: strikes *and* maturities both differ — a hybrid of a price spread and a calendar spread.

## Combinations (calls and puts together)

- **Straddle (bottom/long)**: buy a call and a put, same $$K$$ and $$T$$. Bets on a **big move in either direction** (long volatility). Breakevens at $$K \pm$$ total premium.
- **Strangle**: buy put at $$K_1$$ and call at $$K_2 > K_1$$. Cheaper than a straddle but needs a **bigger** move.
- **Strip**: **1 call + 2 puts** (same $$K, T$$). Big move expected, **down more likely**.
- **Strap**: **2 calls + 1 put**. Big move expected, **up more likely**.
- **Collar (zero-cost)**: long stock + long put $$K_1$$ + short call $$K_2$$, strikes chosen so the call premium pays for the put. Locks profit into the band $$[K_1, K_2]$$ for zero outlay.

{: .warning }
**Payoff vs. profit** is the most common point-loser: profit = payoff − net premium paid (or + net premium received). The wrong answer choice is almost always the right number give or take exactly the net premium — read which quantity is asked. Second trap: **strip vs. strap** — strip = 2 **p**uts (pessimistic tilt, down), strap = 2 calls (up).

### Worked example: bear put spread

Buy a 50-strike put for $5 and sell a 45-strike put for $2 (same expiry). Net debit $$D = 5 - 2 = \$3$$.

| $$S_T$$ | Long 50-put payoff | Short 45-put payoff | Total payoff | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 10 | −5 | 5 | **+2** |
| 45 | 5 | 0 | 5 | **+2** |
| 47 | 3 | 0 | 3 | **0** |
| 48 | 2 | 0 | 2 | −1 |
| 50 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **−3** |
| 55 | 0 | 0 | 0 | −3 |

- Max profit $$= (K_2 - K_1) - D = (50 - 45) - 3 = \$2$$, earned for all $$S_T \le 45$$.
- Max loss $$= D = \$3$$, for all $$S_T \ge 50$$.
- Breakeven $$= K_2 - D = 50 - 3 = \$47$$.

### Worked example: long straddle

Buy a 100-strike call for $6 and a 100-strike put for $4. Total premium $$= \$10$$.

| $$S_T$$ | Call payoff | Put payoff | Total payoff | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 | 0 | 20 | 20 | **+10** |
| 90 | 0 | 10 | 10 | **0** |
| 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **−10** |
| 110 | 10 | 0 | 10 | **0** |
| 120 | 20 | 0 | 20 | +10 |

- Breakevens: $$K - 10 = 90$$ and $$K + 10 = 110$$.
- Max loss $$= \$10$$ at exactly $$S_T = 100$$; profit is unlimited above 110 and grows to $$+90$$ as $$S_T \to 0$$.

## Master summary table

| Strategy | Construction | View | Max profit | Max loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Covered call | Long stock + short call | Mildly bullish, sell upside | $$K - S_0 + c$$ | $$S_0 - c$$ (stock to 0) |
| Protective put | Long stock + long put | Bullish, want insurance | Unlimited | $$S_0 - K + p$$ |
| Bull call spread | +call $$K_1$$, −call $$K_2$$ | Moderately bullish | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ | $$D$$ |
| Bull put spread | +put $$K_1$$, −put $$K_2$$ | Moderately bullish | $$C$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - C$$ |
| Bear call spread | −call $$K_1$$, +call $$K_2$$ | Moderately bearish | $$C$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - C$$ |
| Bear put spread | +put $$K_2$$, −put $$K_1$$ | Moderately bearish | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ | $$D$$ |
| Box spread | Bull call + bear put, same strikes | Pure interest-rate play | $$K_2 - K_1$$ certain | None if bought at PV |
| Long butterfly | +$$K_1$$, −2$$K_2$$, +$$K_3$$ calls | Low volatility near $$K_2$$ | $$K_2 - K_1 - D$$ | $$D$$ |
| Calendar spread | −short-dated, +long-dated, same $$K$$ | $$S_T \approx K$$ at short expiry | Limited | Net debit |
| Long straddle | +call, +put, same $$K$$ | Big move, either way | Unlimited | Total premium |
| Long strangle | +put $$K_1$$, +call $$K_2$$ | Bigger move, either way | Unlimited | Total premium |
| Strip | +1 call, +2 puts | Big move, down likelier | Large (down) | Total premium |
| Strap | +2 calls, +1 put | Big move, up likelier | Unlimited (up) | Total premium |
| Collar | Stock + put $$K_1$$ − call $$K_2$$ | Lock in a band, zero cost | Capped at $$K_2$$ | Floored at $$K_1$$ |

## Quick quiz

1. An investor buys a 30-strike call for $3 and sells a 35-strike call for $1. The maximum profit is:
   - A. $2
   - B. $3
   - C. $5
   - D. Unlimited

2. A box spread with strikes 50 and 55 expires in 1 year; $$r = 5\%$$ continuous. Its no-arbitrage price is closest to:
   - A. $5.00
   - B. $4.76
   - C. $5.26
   - D. $4.52

3. A trader expects a large price move and believes a decline is more likely than a rise. The best-suited strategy is a:
   - A. Strap
   - B. Strip
   - C. Bull put spread
   - D. Calendar spread

4. The profit diagram of a covered call most resembles that of a:
   - A. Long put
   - B. Short put
   - C. Long call
   - D. Short straddle

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. **B** — net debit $$= 3 - 1 = 2$$; max profit $$= (35 - 30) - 2 = \$3$$ (max payoff 5 minus debit 2).
2. **B** — $$(55 - 50)e^{-0.05} = 5(0.9512) = \$4.76$$.
3. **B** — strip = 1 call + 2 puts: profits from a big move, with double weight on the downside.
4. **B** — by put-call parity, long stock + short call = short put + cash.
</details>
