# 12. Options Markets

GARP tests the mechanics: payoffs vs profits for the four basic positions, moneyness and intrinsic/time value, contract specifications and corporate-action adjustments (the split-vs-cash-dividend trap), writer margin formulas, and how warrants, employee stock options, and convertibles differ from exchange-traded options.

## Learning objectives

- Describe call and put options, American and European styles, and the payoff and profit of long and short positions.
- Define moneyness, intrinsic value, and time value.
- Describe exchange-traded option specifications: expiration cycles, strike spacing, contract size, position and exercise limits.
- Explain how stock splits and stock dividends (but not cash dividends) adjust option terms.
- Describe option trading: market makers, bid-ask spreads, and offsetting orders.
- Calculate margin requirements for naked option writers; explain why covered calls require no extra margin.
- Describe the OCC's role, exercise mechanics, and assignment.
- Identify nonstandard products: FLEX options, weeklys, binary options, DOOM options/CEBOs.
- Compare warrants, employee stock options, and convertibles with exchange-traded options.

## Payoffs, profits, and moneyness

A **call** gives the right to buy at strike K; a **put** the right to sell at K. **American** options can be exercised any time up to expiration; **European** only at expiration (most exchange-traded stock options are American). The option buyer pays a premium c (call) or p (put); the writer receives it and bears the obligation.

| Position | Payoff at T | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| Long call | $$\max(S_T - K, 0)$$ | $$\max(S_T - K, 0) - c$$ |
| Short call | $$-\max(S_T - K, 0)$$ | $$c - \max(S_T - K, 0)$$ |
| Long put | $$\max(K - S_T, 0)$$ | $$\max(K - S_T, 0) - p$$ |
| Short put | $$-\max(K - S_T, 0)$$ | $$p - \max(K - S_T, 0)$$ |

Moneyness (S = current stock price):

| | Call | Put |
|---|---|---|
| **In the money (ITM)** | S > K | S < K |
| **At the money (ATM)** | S = K | S = K |
| **Out of the money (OTM)** | S < K | S > K |

**Intrinsic value** = the exercise value now: $$\max(S-K,0)$$ for a call, $$\max(K-S,0)$$ for a put. **Time value** = premium − intrinsic value; it reflects remaining optionality and is largest ATM.

## Contract specifications

- **Contract size**: one contract = options on **100 shares**.
- **Expirations**: standardized monthly cycles (January, February, or March cycle) plus the current and next month; LEAPS extend to ~3 years; weeklys expire each Friday.
- **Strike spacing**: roughly $2.50 intervals for low-priced stocks, $5 for mid, $10 for high-priced (finer near the money in practice).
- **Position limits** cap the contracts one investor (or acting-in-concert group) may hold on the same side of the market; **exercise limits** cap exercises over a window. Both deter market manipulation.

## Corporate-action adjustments

Terms are adjusted for **stock splits and stock dividends**, not for ordinary cash dividends. For an n-for-m split: strike is multiplied by m/n and the number of shares per contract by n/m — the position's economics are preserved.

- 3-for-1 split, call with K = $90 on 100 shares → K becomes 90 × 1/3 = **$30** on **300 shares**.
- 25% stock dividend = 5-for-4 split → K = 90 × 4/5 = **$72** on **125 shares**.

{: .warning }
Exchange-traded options are **not** adjusted for ordinary cash dividends — the stock drops on the ex-date and option holders simply bear it (the expected dividend is priced into the premium). They **are** adjusted for splits and stock dividends. Exams love offering "the strike is reduced by the dividend" as a distractor; only special/large cash distributions trigger discretionary OCC adjustments.

## Trading and the OCC

**Market makers** quote bid and ask continuously (spread is their compensation) and provide liquidity; the exchange caps spread width. Positions are usually closed by an **offsetting order** rather than exercise. The **Options Clearing Corporation (OCC)** is the central counterparty: it guarantees performance, becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, and nets positions. On exercise, the OCC selects a member firm with a short position, which **assigns** one of its customers (random or FIFO). Most ITM options are exercised automatically at expiration.

{: .tip }
Selling an ITM American option usually beats exercising it early: exercise captures only intrinsic value, while a sale also captures remaining time value. Early exercise of a call on a non-dividend stock is never optimal for this reason (formalized in Chapter 13).

## Margin

- Option **buyers** pay the premium **in full** — options already embed leverage, so no credit is extended. Only options with maturity over **9 months** may be bought on margin (50%).
- **Naked writers** post the greater of two amounts (per share, times 100, times contracts):
  1. Premium + 20% of the stock price − amount (if any) the option is OTM
  2. Premium + 10% of the **stock price** (naked calls) or 10% of the **strike** (naked puts)
- **Covered call** writers (long stock, short call) need no margin beyond the stock position — the stock collateralizes the obligation.

{: .note }
Premiums are quoted per share, but cash changes hands per contract: a $3.50 quote costs 3.50 × 100 = $350 per contract. Exam answers are sometimes offered per share and sometimes per contract — read which one the question asks for.

## Nonstandard products

- **FLEX options**: exchange-traded but with negotiable terms (strike, expiration, exercise style) — OTC flexibility with OCC clearing.
- **Weeklys**: standard options expiring each week.
- **Binary (digital) options**: pay a fixed amount if ITM at expiration, else nothing.
- **DOOM options / CEBOs**: deep-out-of-the-money puts and Credit Event Binary Options that pay on a company's collapse or credit event — exchange-traded credit protection, CDS-like.

## Warrants, employee stock options, and convertibles

| Feature | Exchange-traded options | Warrants / ESOs / convertibles |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | Written by investors, cleared by OCC | Issued by the **company itself** |
| Exercise | Writer delivers existing shares | Company issues **new shares** → **dilution** |
| Standardization | Standardized, liquid | Custom terms; ESOs nontransferable, with vesting |
| Number outstanding | Unlimited (open interest varies) | Fixed at issuance |

Convertible bonds embed a call on the issuer's equity inside a bond; conversion also creates new shares. Because exercise brings cash to the company and dilutes existing holders, valuation differs from ordinary options.

### Worked example: profit on a long put

You buy 1 put contract (100 shares), K = $50, premium $3.50 per share. At expiration S = $42.

- Payoff = $$\max(50 - 42, 0)$$ = $8.00 per share
- Profit = 8.00 − 3.50 = $4.50 per share → 4.50 × 100 = **$450 per contract**
- Breakeven: $$S_T = K - p$$ = 50 − 3.50 = $46.50
- Maximum profit (stock to zero): 50 − 3.50 = $46.50 per share; maximum loss: the $350 premium.

### Worked example: naked call writer margin

You write 5 naked calls, K = $55, stock at S = $51, premium $2.00 per share. Per share:

- Formula 1: 2.00 + 0.20 × 51 − (55 − 51) = 2.00 + 10.20 − 4.00 = **$8.20** (the option is $4 OTM)
- Formula 2: 2.00 + 0.10 × 51 = $7.10

Margin = max(8.20, 7.10) × 100 shares × 5 contracts = 8.20 × 500 = **$4,100**. (For naked puts, formula 2 uses 10% of the strike instead of the stock price.)

## Quick quiz

1. A stock declares a $1.00 ordinary cash dividend. An exchange-traded call on the stock with K = $40 will have its terms:
   - A. Adjusted to K = $39
   - B. Adjusted to K = $39 and 100 shares becomes 102.5
   - C. Unchanged
   - D. Adjusted only if the option is in the money

2. You buy a call for $4.00 with K = $40. At expiration the stock is $47. Your profit per share is:
   - A. $3.00
   - B. $4.00
   - C. $7.00
   - D. −$4.00

3. A put with K = $60 trades at $9.50 when the stock is at $52. Its time value is:
   - A. $0.00
   - B. $1.50
   - C. $8.00
   - D. $9.50

4. After a 4-for-1 stock split, a put contract with K = $120 on 100 shares becomes:
   - A. K = $120 on 100 shares (no change)
   - B. K = $30 on 100 shares
   - C. K = $480 on 25 shares
   - D. K = $30 on 400 shares

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. **C** — ordinary cash dividends do not adjust option terms; only splits/stock dividends (and special distributions) do.
2. **A** — payoff 47 − 40 = 7, minus the $4 premium = $3.00.
3. **B** — intrinsic = 60 − 52 = 8.00; time value = 9.50 − 8.00 = 1.50.
4. **D** — n-for-m = 4-for-1: strike × 1/4 = 30, shares × 4 = 400; economics preserved.
</details>
