**Q1.** A GARP member learns that part of her bonus depends on the profits of the trading desk whose risk she independently reports on. Under the Code, she must:
- A. Resign from her position immediately
- B. Quietly decline the bonus and say nothing
- C. Fully disclose the conflict of interest to all affected parties
- D. Keep silent unless someone specifically asks her

**Answer: C**

The question asks what the Code requires when a member faces a conflict of interest.

The GARP Code's main remedy for conflicts of interest is FULL DISCLOSURE to all affected parties — not necessarily avoiding the situation. By telling everyone affected, the member lets them judge her objectivity with the conflict in plain view. So C is correct.

Analogy: a referee who is also a fan of one team should announce it openly, so everyone knows to watch for bias — not just secretly try to be fair.

- A is wrong: the Code does not require resigning over every conflict; disclosure is the operative duty.
- B is wrong: declining the bonus silently does not satisfy the duty to disclose the conflict to affected parties.
- D is wrong: staying silent until asked is the opposite of full, proactive disclosure.

📌 Exam tip: The Code says DISCLOSE conflicts (fully, to all affected parties) — it does NOT say "avoid all conflicts." Disclosure beats silence.

**Q2.** Under the GARP Code, a member may reveal confidential client information when:
- A. The information would benefit some of the member's other clients
- B. Disclosure is required by law or legal/regulatory process
- C. The client relationship has simply ended
- D. The information is more than five years old

**Answer: B**

The question asks for a legitimate exception to confidentiality.

Confidentiality under the Code is strong but NOT absolute. A member may disclose confidential information in two situations: when the client has given consent, or when disclosure is required by law (such as a valid subpoena or regulatory process). So B is correct.

Analogy: a doctor keeps your records private, but if a court lawfully orders them, the doctor must comply — the law overrides the privacy duty.

- A is wrong: helping other clients is not a valid reason; that would actually be misusing confidential information.
- C is wrong: simply ending the relationship does not free the member to reveal old confidential information.
- D is wrong: there is no "five-year expiry" on confidentiality in the Code.

📌 Exam tip: Confidentiality exceptions = (1) client consent, or (2) disclosure required by law. Nothing about age of data, ended relationships, or benefiting others.

**Q3.** Which is a possible consequence of violating the GARP Code of Conduct?
- A. Automatic criminal prosecution carried out by GARP itself
- B. Suspension or revocation of the right to use the FRM designation
- C. A fine payable to GARP of up to $1 million
- D. Mandatory retraining administered by the member's employer

**Answer: B**

The question asks what GARP can actually do when the Code is violated.

GARP can investigate violations under its disciplinary procedures and impose sanctions such as suspending or revoking membership and the right to use the FRM (or other GARP) designation; candidates can be barred from the exam program. So B is correct.

Analogy: a sports association can ban a player or strip their title, but it cannot send anyone to jail — courts do that.

- A is wrong: GARP does not prosecute crimes; violations of law are referred to legal/regulatory authorities, who handle prosecution.
- C is wrong: GARP does not levy a $1 million fine; its sanctions concern membership and designation rights.
- D is wrong: GARP does not order an employer to retrain the member; that is not among its sanctions.

📌 Exam tip: GARP sanctions = suspend/revoke membership and designation, or bar candidates. GARP does NOT impose criminal penalties or fines — the law handles crimes separately.

**Q4.** A member presents a credit model's outputs to clients as "precise default probabilities," even though the model has wide confidence bands. This most directly violates the duty to:
- A. Maintain confidentiality
- B. Disclose conflicts of interest
- C. Not overstate accuracy and to communicate model limitations clearly
- D. Nothing — marketing language is always exempt

**Answer: C**

The question asks which Code duty is broken by overstating how accurate a model is.

The Code requires members to be diligent about NOT overstating the accuracy or certainty of their results, and to make assumptions, limitations, and the degree of uncertainty transparent. Calling fuzzy estimates "precise" hides the real uncertainty, so it violates the duty to communicate clearly and not overstate accuracy. So C is correct.

Analogy: it's like a weather app saying "0% chance of rain, guaranteed" when the forecast is actually quite uncertain — that misleads people into bad decisions.

- A is wrong: nothing confidential was revealed, so confidentiality isn't the issue.
- B is wrong: there is no conflict of interest here; the issue is misrepresenting accuracy.
- D is wrong: marketing language is NOT exempt; members must communicate honestly about uncertainty.

📌 Exam tip: Two recurring Code themes: be transparent about model limitations (never overstate accuracy) and fully disclose conflicts. When two answers look ethical, pick the one favoring disclosure and honest uncertainty.

**Q5.** The GARP Code of Conduct applies to:
- A. Only individuals who currently hold the full FRM certification
- B. GARP members, GARP certification holders, AND candidates registered for GARP exams
- C. Only employees who hold a formal "risk manager" job title
- D. Only members who have agreed to it in writing each year

**Answer: B**

The question asks who is bound by the Code.

The Code binds GARP members, holders of GARP certifications (such as the FRM), AND candidates who have registered for GARP exams. By registering for an exam, a candidate accepts the Code. So B is correct.

Analogy: signing up for the race means you agree to the race rules — you don't have to have finished and won a medal first.

- A is wrong: it covers more than just certified FRMs; candidates are bound too.
- C is wrong: the Code applies to professional conduct generally, not only to people with a "risk manager" title.
- D is wrong: acceptance happens upon registration; there is no requirement to re-sign it in writing every year.

📌 Exam tip: The Code binds candidates FROM the moment of exam registration — not only finished, certified FRMs.

**Q6.** A member is working in a country whose local practice allows accepting gifts that the GARP Code would prohibit. The member should:
- A. Accept the gifts, since local practice always governs
- B. Follow the stricter standard, declining gifts that could compromise independence
- C. Accept the gifts but disclose them only if caught
- D. Ignore the Code because it does not apply abroad

**Answer: B**

The question asks how to handle a clash between lax local practice and the stricter Code.

The Code requires members to comply with it even where local law or practice is laxer; where they conflict, the member should follow the stricter standard consistent with law. "My country allows it" is not a defense for conduct the Code prohibits. Here, accepting gifts that could compromise independence is barred, so the member declines. So B is correct.

Analogy: even if a friend's house has no curfew, your own family's stricter curfew still applies to you when you're out.

- A is wrong: local practice does not override the Code; the stricter standard wins.
- C is wrong: "disclose only if caught" is not honest disclosure and still violates the gift rule.
- D is wrong: the Code applies to members' professional conduct generally, including abroad.

📌 Exam tip: When local rules are laxer than the Code, follow the STRICTER standard. "It's allowed here" is never a valid defense under the Code.

**Q7.** A risk manager presents a value-at-risk (VaR) figure to the board but does not mention that the model excludes the firm's largest illiquid position. This most directly violates:
- A. The duty of confidentiality
- B. The duty to use generally accepted practices and communicate risk clearly, accurately, and completely
- C. Conflict-of-interest disclosure rules
- D. Nothing, because VaR is only an estimate anyway

**Answer: B**

The question asks which Code duty is breached by leaving out a key limitation of a risk figure.

The Code requires members to communicate risk information clearly, accurately, and not misleadingly — including making the limitations and assumptions of models transparent. Hiding that the VaR omits the firm's largest illiquid position makes the figure misleading and breaks the duty to adhere to generally accepted practices in risk communication. So B is correct.

Analogy: showing your boss a budget that quietly leaves out the biggest expense makes the whole picture misleading, even if every other number is right.

- A is wrong: no confidential information was disclosed; that's not the issue.
- C is wrong: there's no conflict of interest here, just incomplete risk communication.
- D is wrong: VaR being an estimate is no excuse for hiding a material limitation; the omission is the violation.

📌 Exam tip: Risk communications must be clear, accurate, complete, and upfront about limitations and assumptions. Omitting a material exclusion is misleading.

**Q8.** A consultant recommends a particular risk-software vendor to a client but does not mention that the vendor pays him a referral fee. Under the Code, this is a failure to:
- A. Maintain professional competence
- B. Disclose a conflict of interest
- C. Keep client information confidential
- D. Comply with local tax law

**Answer: B**

The question asks which duty the consultant breaks by hiding the referral fee.

The referral fee gives the consultant a personal incentive to recommend that vendor regardless of what's best for the client — an actual or potential conflict of interest. The Code requires fully disclosing such conflicts to all affected parties. By staying silent, he fails to disclose a conflict of interest. So B is correct.

Analogy: a "friend" who recommends a mechanic should mention if the mechanic slips him cash for every referral — otherwise you can't judge whether the advice is honest.

- A is wrong: this isn't about lacking skill or competence; it's about the hidden incentive.
- C is wrong: no confidential information was leaked.
- D is wrong: the issue is the undisclosed conflict, not tax compliance.

📌 Exam tip: Any payment or benefit that could bias a recommendation is a conflict of interest that must be DISCLOSED to all affected parties.

**Q9.** An FRM candidate who has passed Part I but not yet Part II describes herself on her resume as "FRM certified." Under the Code, this is:
- A. Acceptable, since she passed Part I
- B. A misrepresentation of qualifications, violating professional integrity
- C. A confidentiality violation
- D. Acceptable, because resumes are personal documents

**Answer: B**

The question asks how the Code treats claiming the FRM designation before earning it.

The Code prohibits knowingly misrepresenting qualifications or the meaning of the FRM designation. Calling herself "FRM certified" before completing the full program (and satisfying experience requirements) is a false statement about her qualifications, which violates professional integrity. So B is correct.

Analogy: a medical student who passed only the first set of exams cannot call themselves "Dr." yet — claiming the title early is a misrepresentation.

- A is wrong: passing Part I does not make someone FRM certified; the claim is still false.
- C is wrong: nothing confidential is involved; the issue is a false qualification claim.
- D is wrong: a resume being personal does not exempt it from the truthfulness rules of the Code.

📌 Exam tip: Never overstate your designation status. Misstating qualifications or the meaning of the FRM is a professional-integrity violation.

**Q10.** A member receives a valid legal subpoena ordering the production of certain client records and complies with it. Under the Code, this is:
- A. A confidentiality violation, because client information was disclosed
- B. Not a violation, because disclosure required by law is a permitted exception
- C. A conflict-of-interest violation
- D. A violation only if the client objects

**Answer: B**

The question asks whether obeying a lawful subpoena breaks the confidentiality rule.

The Code's confidentiality duty has an explicit exception: disclosure that is required by law or legal/regulatory process is permitted. A valid subpoena is exactly such a legal requirement, so complying with it does NOT violate the Code. So B is correct.

Analogy: keeping a secret is the rule, but if a judge lawfully orders you to testify, telling the truth in court is allowed — even required.

- A is wrong: disclosure compelled by law is the recognized exception, so it is not a violation.
- C is wrong: there's no conflict of interest involved in complying with a subpoena.
- D is wrong: a client's objection does not override a valid legal requirement to disclose.

📌 Exam tip: Complying with a lawful subpoena or legal process is a permitted exception to confidentiality — not a Code violation.
