**Q1.** What new assumption does multiple regression add beyond the single-variable case?
> **A:** No perfect multicollinearity — no regressor can be an exact linear combination of the others, or OLS cannot separate their effects.

**Q2.** How do you interpret a coefficient $$\beta_j$$ in multiple regression?
> **A:** Expected change in $$Y$$ per one-unit change in $$X_j$$, holding all other regressors constant (a partial / ceteris paribus effect).

**Q3.** What is the dummy variable trap and the fix?
> **A:** Including dummies for every category plus an intercept creates perfect collinearity (dummies sum to the constant). Fix: omit one category as the base.

**Q4.** What is omitted variable bias?
> **A:** Omitting a relevant variable correlated with an included regressor biases the included coefficient — it picks up the omitted variable's effect.

**Q5.** Why can't $$R^2$$ compare models of different sizes, and what fixes it?
> **A:** $$R^2$$ never falls when a regressor is added (even a useless one). Adjusted $$R^2$$ penalizes parameters: $$\bar R^2 = 1 - (1-R^2)\dfrac{n-1}{n-k-1}$$.

**Q6.** When does adding a variable raise adjusted $$R^2$$, and is that the same as significance?
> **A:** It rises only if the new variable's $$\lvert t\rvert > 1$$ — a weaker hurdle than 5% significance ($$\lvert t\rvert > 1.96$$). So a higher $$\bar R^2$$ does NOT mean the variable is statistically significant.

**Q7.** Give the general F-statistic for $$q$$ joint restrictions.
> **A:** $$F = \dfrac{(RSS_r - RSS_u)/q}{RSS_u/(n - k_u - 1)} \sim F_{q,\,n-k_u-1}$$. Always an upper-tail test.

**Q8.** Give the F-statistic for the all-slopes-zero null in terms of $$R^2$$.
> **A:** $$F = \dfrac{R^2/k}{(1-R^2)/(n-k-1)}$$.

**Q9.** $$n = 44$$, $$k = 3$$, $$R^2 = 0.30$$. Compute the overall F (crit $$F_{3,40} \approx 2.84$$).
> **A:** $$F = \dfrac{0.30/3}{0.70/40} = \dfrac{0.10}{0.0175} = 5.71 > 2.84$$ → reject; regressors jointly significant.

**Q10.** $$n = 66$$, $$k = 5$$, $$R^2 = 0.40$$. Compute adjusted $$R^2$$.
> **A:** $$\bar R^2 = 1 - 0.60 \times \dfrac{65}{60} = 1 - 0.65 = 0.35$$.

**Q11.** What does a significant overall F with no significant individual t-statistics indicate?
> **A:** Multicollinearity — correlated regressors matter as a group but inflate individual SEs, so their separate effects are imprecise.

**Q12.** Unrestricted $$RSS_u = 270$$ ($$k_u=4$$, $$n=60$$); restricted $$RSS_r = 306$$ dropping 2 regressors. Compute the F ($$q=2$$; crit $$F_{2,55}\approx 3.16$$).
> **A:** $$F = \dfrac{(306-270)/2}{270/55} = \dfrac{18}{4.909} = 3.67 > 3.16$$ → reject; the pair belongs in the model.

**Q13.** Does a high $$R^2$$ prove causality or correct specification?
> **A:** No — a high $$R^2$$ proves neither causality nor correct specification.
