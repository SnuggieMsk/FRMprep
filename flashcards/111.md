**Q1.** Who is bound by the GARP Code of Conduct?
> **A:** GARP members, holders of GARP certifications (FRM, ERP/SCR), and candidates registered for GARP exams. By registering you accept the Code.

**Q2.** Exam trap: does the Code only apply to formal "risk manager" roles?
> **A:** No. It applies to professional conduct generally — and members must comply even where local law or practice is laxer (where they conflict, follow the stricter standard consistent with law).

**Q3.** What does the conflicts-of-interest principle require — avoidance or disclosure?
> **A:** Disclosure. Members must act fairly and fully disclose any actual or potential conflict to all affected parties. Disclosure, not necessarily avoidance, is the operative duty.

**Q4.** When may a member reveal confidential information?
> **A:** Only with consent, or where disclosure is required by law or legal/regulatory process. Confidential information must never be used for personal benefit. Confidentiality is not absolute.

**Q5.** What does the professional-integrity principle require regarding statements and qualifications?
> **A:** Not knowingly misrepresent facts or make false/misleading statements — including about one's own or others' qualifications and the meaning of the FRM designation.

**Q6.** What does integrity require regarding gifts and independence?
> **A:** Exercise reasonable judgment and maintain independence; do not offer, solicit, or accept gifts/benefits that could reasonably be expected to compromise (or appear to compromise) independence.

**Q7.** What does "adherence to generally accepted practices" require for communicating risk?
> **A:** Communications of risk information must be clear, accurate, and not misleading — making assumptions, limitations, and the degree of model/analysis uncertainty transparent.

**Q8.** What two themes does the Code repeat?
> **A:** Transparency about limitations (never overstate model accuracy or hide assumptions) and disclosure (conflicts fully disclosed to all affected parties).

**Q9.** Under fundamental responsibilities, what must members do regarding results?
> **A:** Be diligent about not overstating the accuracy or certainty of results or conclusions, and distinguish fact from opinion in analysis and recommendations.

**Q10.** What are the potential consequences of violating the Code?
> **A:** Formal investigation; suspension or revocation of GARP membership and the right to use the FRM (or other GARP) designation; candidates barred from the exam program; plus reputational censure.

**Q11.** Does GARP impose criminal penalties or fines?
> **A:** No. GARP can suspend/revoke membership and designation rights, but violations of law are referred to legal/regulatory authorities — the Code does not shield anyone from the law, nor levy criminal penalties or fines.

**Q12.** Trap: can a candidate awaiting Part II results call herself "FRM certified"?
> **A:** No — that misrepresents qualifications and violates professional integrity. The designation is not held until fully certified.

**Q13.** Is "my country's rules allow it" a valid defense?
> **A:** No. It is not a defense for conduct the Code prohibits; members follow the stricter of the Code and applicable law.

**Q14.** A risk manager presents VaR to the board without noting the model excludes the largest illiquid position. Which principle is violated?
> **A:** Adherence to generally accepted practices — risk communication must be clear, accurate, complete, and disclose limitations.
