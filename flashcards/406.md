**Q1.** Distinguish expected loss, unexpected loss, and economic capital.
> **A:** EL = mean of the loss distribution (a cost of business, covered by pricing/provisions). UL = dispersion around the mean (covered by capital). Economic capital = credit VaR = $$\text{WCL}_{99.9\%} - EL$$.

**Q2.** Expected loss formula.
> **A:** $$EL = PD \times LGD \times EAD$$, where LGD = 1 − recovery rate, EAD = exposure at default. Portfolio EL = simple sum of individual ELs (no correlation needed — expectation is linear).

**Q3.** Compute EL: $25M exposure, PD 1.5%, recovery 55%.
> **A:** LGD = 0.45, so $$EL = 0.015 \times 0.45 \times 25M = \$168{,}750$$. (Using 55% as LGD is the recovery/LGD flip trap.)

**Q4.** Why is portfolio UL not the sum of individual ULs?
> **A:** It aggregates with default correlations: $$UL_P = \sqrt{\sum_i\sum_j \rho_{ij}UL_iUL_j}$$, far below the sum for low correlations. Each loan's risk contribution depends on its correlation with the portfolio.

**Q5.** Write the single-factor (Vasicek/Gaussian copula) latent variable.
> **A:** $$X_i = \sqrt{\rho}\,F + \sqrt{1-\rho}\,\epsilon_i$$, where F is the common systematic factor and ε the idiosyncratic shock; default when X falls below a threshold.

**Q6.** Worst-case default rate (WCDR) formula.
> **A:** $$WCDR = N\!\left(\frac{N^{-1}(PD) + \sqrt{\rho}\,N^{-1}(X)}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\right)$$. Basel uses X = 99.9%, $$N^{-1}(0.999)=3.090$$. With ρ = 0, WCDR = PD.

**Q7.** Credit VaR for a granular portfolio.
> **A:** $$\text{Credit VaR} = EAD \times LGD \times (WCDR - PD)$$. Subtract PD because capital covers unexpected loss only. This is the Basel IRB capital formula (plus a maturity adjustment).

**Q8.** Compute WCDR: PD = 1%, ρ = 0.1, 99.9%.
> **A:** $$N\!\left(\frac{-2.326 + \sqrt{0.1}(3.090)}{\sqrt{0.9}}\right) = N\!\left(\frac{-1.349}{0.9487}\right) = N(-1.422) = 7.75\%$$.

**Q9.** Then compute credit VaR: $100M, LGD 60%, PD 1%, WCDR 7.75%.
> **A:** $$100M \times 0.60 \times (0.0775-0.01) = 100M \times 0.60 \times 0.0675 = \$4.05M$$.

**Q10.** What is the Merton model's view of equity?
> **A:** Equity is a call option on firm assets with strike = debt face value D maturing at T. Equity holders get $$\max(V_T-D,0)$$; debt holders get $$\min(V_T,D)$$. Risky debt = risk-free debt − put on assets.

**Q11.** Define distance to default in the Merton/KMV framework.
> **A:** $$DD = \frac{\ln(V/D) + (\mu - \sigma_V^2/2)T}{\sigma_V\sqrt{T}}$$. V and σ_V are backed out from observed equity value and equity volatility; DD is mapped to a PD empirically.

**Q12.** Strengths and weaknesses of the Merton model.
> **A:** Strengths: forward-looking, updates continuously with the stock price. Weaknesses: needs traded equity, assumes a simple debt structure and single default date, normality understates tail PDs.

**Q13.** What drives the thickness of the credit loss tail?
> **A:** Default correlation (ρ), concentration/granularity (single-name and sector), and LGD uncertainty (and its adverse correlation with default rates). Credit losses are highly skewed.
