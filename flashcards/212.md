**Q1.** Give the simple and log return formulas and their relationship.
> **A:** $$R_t = \dfrac{P_t - P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}$$; $$r_t = \ln\!\left(\dfrac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right) = \ln(1+R_t)$$. Always $$r_t \le R_t$$, near-equal for small returns.

**Q2.** When do log returns vs. simple returns aggregate?
> **A:** Log returns add across TIME ($$r_{0,T} = \sum_t r_t$$); simple returns aggregate across ASSETS (portfolio return = weighted average of component simple returns).

**Q3.** Give the annualization formulas for volatility (252 trading days).
> **A:** $$\sigma_{\text{annual}} = \sigma_{\text{daily}}\sqrt{252}$$; $$\sigma_{\text{monthly}} = \sigma_{\text{annual}}/\sqrt{12}$$ — variance scales with time under iid.

**Q4.** When does the $$\sqrt{t}$$ rule under- or over-state long-horizon risk?
> **A:** Positive autocorrelation ⇒ understates risk; mean reversion ⇒ overstates risk. With GARCH clustering, scaling today's high vol by $$\sqrt{t}$$ ignores reversion to the long-run level.

**Q5.** What is implied volatility and how does it differ from historical volatility?
> **A:** The $$\sigma$$ that sets a Black–Scholes–Merton price equal to the observed market option price — forward-looking and risk-neutral, unlike backward-looking historical estimates. The smile/skew shows the market rejects pure lognormality.

**Q6.** State the EWMA variance update and its key property.
> **A:** $$\sigma_t^2 = \lambda\sigma_{t-1}^2 + (1-\lambda)r_{t-1}^2$$ (RiskMetrics $$\lambda = 0.94$$). Weights decay geometrically; higher $$\lambda$$ = smoother. EWMA has NO mean reversion.

**Q7.** State the GARCH(1,1) model and its stationarity condition.
> **A:** $$\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta\sigma_{t-1}^2$$, covariance-stationary if $$\alpha + \beta < 1$$.

**Q8.** Give the GARCH(1,1) long-run variance and how forecasts converge.
> **A:** $$\sigma_{LR}^2 = \dfrac{\omega}{1 - \alpha - \beta}$$; multi-step forecasts converge to it geometrically at rate $$(\alpha+\beta)^h$$.

**Q9.** How is EWMA a special case of GARCH(1,1)?
> **A:** Set $$\omega = 0$$, $$\alpha = 1-\lambda$$, $$\beta = \lambda$$; then $$\alpha + \beta = 1$$ (integrated, no mean reversion).

**Q10.** EWMA update: $$\lambda = 0.94$$, $$\sigma_{t-1} = 1.0\%$$, $$r_{t-1} = 2.0\%$$. Find $$\sigma_t$$.
> **A:** $$\sigma_t^2 = 0.94(0.01)^2 + 0.06(0.02)^2 = 0.000118$$; $$\sigma_t = 1.086\%$$.

**Q11.** GARCH: $$\omega = 0.000003$$, $$\alpha = 0.08$$, $$\beta = 0.90$$. Find the long-run daily volatility.
> **A:** $$\sigma_{LR}^2 = 0.000003/(1-0.98) = 0.00015$$; $$\sigma_{LR} = 1.22\%$$.

**Q12.** Compare Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall dependence measures.
> **A:** Pearson: linear association, not robust, not invariant to nonlinear transforms. Spearman (correlation of ranks) and Kendall (concordance of pairs): capture monotonic association, robust to outliers, invariant to monotone transforms.

**Q13.** Give the Spearman (no ties) and Kendall formulas.
> **A:** $$\rho_s = 1 - \dfrac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}$$ ($$d_i$$ = rank differences); $$\tau = \dfrac{n_c - n_d}{\binom{n}{2}}$$.

**Q14.** A price falls from $50 to $45. Find the log return.
> **A:** $$\ln(45/50) = \ln(0.9) = -10.54\%$$ (the simple return is $$-10.00\%$$).
