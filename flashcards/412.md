**Q1.** What is the one-factor assumption behind DV01, duration, and convexity?
> **A:** Rate changes are driven by one factor — every yield moves by the same (parallel) shift. The metrics say nothing about steepeners, flatteners, or twists.

**Q2.** Define DV01.
> **A:** The dollar price change for a one-basis-point decline in yield: $$\text{DV01} = -\frac{\Delta P}{10{,}000\cdot\Delta y}$$ (Δy in decimal). Positive for ordinary long bonds.

**Q3.** DV01 hedge ratio.
> **A:** $$N = -\frac{\text{DV01}_{position}}{\text{DV01}_{hedge}}$$. The minus sign means the hedge takes the opposite side. Combined DV01 is zero only for a parallel 1bp move.

**Q4.** Hedge a portfolio with DV01 = $12,500 using futures with DV01 = $85/contract.
> **A:** $$N = -\frac{12{,}500}{85} = -147.06$$ → sell 147 contracts.

**Q5.** Define Macaulay duration.
> **A:** Value-weighted average time to cash flows: $$D_{Mac} = \frac{\sum_t t\cdot PV(CF_t)}{P}$$. The horizon where price and reinvestment risk offset; for a zero, $$D_{Mac}=T$$.

**Q6.** Modified duration and the price-change approximation.
> **A:** $$D_{Mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1+y/m}$$; $$\frac{\Delta P}{P}\approx -D_{Mod}\Delta y$$. With continuous compounding, Macaulay = modified.

**Q7.** When must effective duration be used, and its formula?
> **A:** For bonds whose cash flows change with rates (callables, putables, MBS). $$D_{Eff} = \frac{P_- - P_+}{2P\Delta y}$$. For option-free bonds, effective ≈ modified.

**Q8.** Relate DV01 to dollar duration.
> **A:** $$DD = D_{Mod}\cdot P$$, so $$\text{DV01} = \frac{D_{Mod}\cdot P}{10{,}000}$$ — dollar duration per basis point.

**Q9.** Duration-plus-convexity price-change estimate.
> **A:** $$\Delta P \approx -D\cdot P\cdot\Delta y + \frac{1}{2}C\cdot P\cdot(\Delta y)^2$$. Effective convexity: $$C_{Eff} = \frac{P_+ + P_- - 2P}{P(\Delta y)^2}$$.

**Q10.** Estimate ΔP: P = $100, D_Mod = 7.0, C = 60, rates rise 100bp.
> **A:** $$-7.0(100)(0.01) + \frac{1}{2}(60)(100)(0.01)^2 = -7.00 + 0.30 = -\$6.70$$. Positive convexity cushions losses, amplifies gains.

**Q11.** Duration ranking intuition.
> **A:** Longer maturity → higher duration; lower coupon → higher duration; lower yield → higher duration. A zero is the max-duration bond for its maturity.

**Q12.** How do portfolio duration and convexity aggregate?
> **A:** Value-weighted averages: $$D_{port} = \sum_i \frac{P_i}{P}D_i$$, $$C_{port}=\sum_i\frac{P_i}{P}C_i$$. DV01s simply add. Holds exactly only under parallel shifts.

**Q13.** Barbell vs bullet with equal duration: which has more convexity and which the higher yield?
> **A:** The barbell has more convexity (cash flows more dispersed) and outperforms in large parallel moves either direction; convexity isn't free, so the barbell usually carries the lower yield (bullet higher).
