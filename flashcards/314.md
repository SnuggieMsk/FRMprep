**Q1.** Covered call: construction, motivation, and parity link
> **A:** Long stock + short call. Earns the premium, gives up upside above $$K$$ (mildly bullish). By put-call parity $$S_0 - c = Ke^{-rT} - p$$, so its profit profile looks like a SHORT PUT.

**Q2.** Protective put: construction, motivation, and parity link
> **A:** Long stock + long put. Insurance against a fall (bullish, wants downside protection). By parity $$S_0 + p = c + Ke^{-rT}$$, so its profile looks like a LONG CALL.

**Q3.** Principal-protected note (PPN) construction and conditions
> **A:** A zero-coupon bond + a call option, returning principal at maturity plus equity upside. Hard to create when interest rates are LOW (the zero absorbs nearly all principal, little left for the option) or volatility is HIGH (the call is expensive). Issuers cheapen the option via higher strikes, caps, averaging, or knock-outs.

**Q4.** Bull spreads: construction and key numbers ($$K_1 < K_2$$)
> **A:** Bull call (debit $$D$$): buy call $$K_1$$, sell call $$K_2$$; max profit $$K_2-K_1-D$$, max loss $$D$$, breakeven $$K_1+D$$. Bull put (credit $$C$$): buy put $$K_1$$, sell put $$K_2$$; max profit $$C$$, max loss $$K_2-K_1-C$$, breakeven $$K_2-C$$.

**Q5.** Bear spreads: construction and key numbers ($$K_1 < K_2$$)
> **A:** Bear call (credit $$C$$): sell call $$K_1$$, buy call $$K_2$$; max profit $$C$$, max loss $$K_2-K_1-C$$, breakeven $$K_1+C$$. Bear put (debit $$D$$): buy put $$K_2$$, sell put $$K_1$$; max profit $$K_2-K_1-D$$, max loss $$D$$, breakeven $$K_2-D$$.

**Q6.** Box spread and its no-arbitrage value
> **A:** Bull call spread + bear put spread on the same $$K_1, K_2$$. Payoff is always $$K_2-K_1$$, so fair value = PV = $$(K_2-K_1)e^{-rT}$$ — a pure interest-rate play. Any other price is arbitrage, but only with EUROPEAN options (American early exercise breaks the box).

**Q7.** Long butterfly: construction and view
> **A:** Buy calls $$K_1$$ and $$K_3$$, sell 2 calls $$K_2$$ (debit $$D$$). Bets $$S_T$$ stays near $$K_2$$ (short volatility), capped risk either way. Max profit $$K_2-K_1-D$$ at $$S_T=K_2$$; max loss $$D$$; breakevens $$K_1+D$$ and $$K_3-D$$.

**Q8.** Calendar vs diagonal spreads
> **A:** Calendar: sell a short-dated call, buy a long-dated call, SAME strike. Profits if $$S_T \approx K$$ at the short maturity (short option dies, long keeps time value); loses on a big move either way. Diagonal: strikes AND maturities both differ — a hybrid.

**Q9.** Straddle vs strangle
> **A:** Long straddle: buy a call and a put, same $$K$$ and $$T$$ — bets on a big move either direction (long volatility), breakevens at $$K \pm$$ total premium. Strangle: buy put $$K_1$$ and call $$K_2 > K_1$$ — cheaper than a straddle but needs a bigger move.

**Q10.** Strip vs strap
> **A:** Strip = 1 call + 2 puts (big move expected, DOWN more likely). Strap = 2 calls + 1 put (big move expected, UP more likely). Same $$K, T$$.

**Q11.** Zero-cost collar
> **A:** Long stock + long put $$K_1$$ + short call $$K_2$$, with strikes chosen so the call premium pays for the put. Locks profit into the band $$[K_1, K_2]$$ for zero outlay; capped at $$K_2$$, floored at $$K_1$$.

**Q12.** Trap: payoff vs profit
> **A:** Profit = payoff − net premium paid (or + net premium received). The wrong answer choice is almost always the right number off by exactly the net premium — read which quantity is asked. Also watch strip (2 puts, down) vs strap (2 calls, up).

**Q13.** Worked: bear put spread numbers
> **A:** Buy 50-put for $5, sell 45-put for $2; net debit $$D=\$3$$. Max profit $$=(50-45)-3=\$2$$ for $$S_T \le 45$$. Max loss $$=D=\$3$$ for $$S_T \ge 50$$. Breakeven $$=50-3=\$47$$.

**Q14.** Worked: long straddle numbers
> **A:** Buy 100-call for $6 and 100-put for $4; total premium $10. Breakevens at $$100-10=90$$ and $$100+10=110$$. Max loss $10 at exactly $$S_T=100$$; profit unlimited above 110 and grows to $$+90$$ as $$S_T \to 0$$.
