**Q1.** What is "risk" in risk management terms?
> **A:** The variability (uncertainty) of outcomes, especially adverse ones — not the expected cost itself. A cost known with certainty is an expense, not a risk.

**Q2.** Is risk management about minimizing or eliminating risk?
> **A:** No. It is about understanding exposures, deciding which risks to retain (because the firm is paid to bear them) versus lay off, and keeping retained risks within a deliberate risk appetite.

**Q3.** What are the five steps of the risk management process?
> **A:** (1) Identify, (2) Analyze and measure, (3) Assess the impact, (4) Manage (avoid, retain/price, mitigate, transfer), (5) Monitor and report.

**Q4.** Formula for expected loss (credit)?
> **A:** $$EL = PD \times LGD \times EAD$$ where PD = probability of default, LGD = loss given default (1 − recovery), EAD = exposure at default.

**Q5.** Expected loss vs. unexpected loss — key difference?
> **A:** EL is the average anticipated loss, a priced-in cost covered by reserves. UL is the variability of loss around the expected level — the genuine risk, covered by capital.

**Q6.** Classic trap: is expected loss a "risk"?
> **A:** No. EL is an anticipated, priced-in cost. Risk (and capital) is about unexpected loss. UL also rises with default correlation, not just individual PDs.

**Q7.** Funding liquidity risk vs. market (trading) liquidity risk?
> **A:** Funding: cannot roll/raise cash to meet obligations (e.g., bank can't roll commercial paper). Market: cannot exit a position without moving the price (e.g., fire-sale of an illiquid bond).

**Q8.** VaR vs. expected shortfall?
> **A:** VaR is a loss threshold not exceeded at a given confidence over a horizon. Expected shortfall averages losses beyond that threshold, capturing tail severity that VaR ignores.

**Q9.** Define moral hazard, adverse selection, and asymmetric information.
> **A:** Moral hazard: a protected party takes more risk (trader with bonus upside, no clawback). Adverse selection: riskiest customers most eager to buy at a given price. Asymmetric information: agents know more than principals.

**Q10.** Name the key risk classes in the GARP taxonomy.
> **A:** Market, credit, liquidity (funding + market), operational, business/strategic, and reputation risk.

**Q11.** What is operational risk?
> **A:** Loss from failed people, processes, systems, or external events — fraud, rogue trading, cyber attacks, IT failure, legal risk, and model risk (often classed here).

**Q12.** Why is reputation risk often called "consequential"?
> **A:** It is typically triggered by another risk event (fraud, mis-selling), causing loss of franchise value from damaged trust.

**Q13.** What happens to correlations during a crisis, and why does that matter for aggregation?
> **A:** Correlations are unstable and rise toward 1 in crises, so diversification and netting benefits assumed in calm markets can vanish exactly when needed — silos understate firm-wide risk.

**Q14.** Give an example of risks interacting rather than adding.
> **A:** Market losses trigger margin calls (funding liquidity risk), forcing fire sales that cause more market losses — the loss spiral of 2008.
