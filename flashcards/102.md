**Q1.** What are the four risk management strategies?
> **A:** Accept (retain), Avoid, Mitigate, and Transfer. Choice flows from comparing the risk to risk appetite and asking whether the firm is paid to hold it.

**Q2.** When is "accept (retain)" the appropriate strategy?
> **A:** When the risk is core to the business model and the firm has an edge in bearing it (e.g., a bank retaining credit risk it can assess), pricing it and holding capital against it.

**Q3.** Risk appetite vs. risk capacity vs. risk profile?
> **A:** Appetite: amount/types of risk the firm is willing to take (board-set). Capacity: the maximum it can absorb. Profile: the risk actually taken at a point in time. Appetite should sit within capacity.

**Q4.** What is the bridge from board strategy to desk-level limits?
> **A:** The risk appetite statement (qualitative narrative plus quantitative limits) cascaded into the limit framework.

**Q5.** Under Modigliani–Miller, does hedging add value?
> **A:** No. In a frictionless world (no taxes, no bankruptcy costs) hedging adds no value — shareholders can hedge on their own account. Value comes only from real-world frictions.

**Q6.** What is the strongest theoretical argument that hedging adds shareholder value?
> **A:** It reduces the probability and cost of financial distress and bankruptcy (also: lowers expected taxes under convex schedules, avoids underinvestment, stabilizes cash flow).

**Q7.** Name key arguments against hedging.
> **A:** Shareholders can hedge themselves more cheaply; direct/indirect costs; hedges can mask performance; a mis-sized hedge becomes speculation; hedging expected, priced risks destroys value.

**Q8.** What is basis risk in a hedge?
> **A:** The hedge instrument does not track the exposure perfectly — differences in grade, location, maturity, or index.

**Q9.** Exam trap: can a sound hedge increase reported earnings volatility?
> **A:** Yes. Derivatives mark to market through earnings while the hedged item accrues — creating reported earnings volatility even when the economic hedge is sound. Don't equate "more volatile earnings" with "hedge failed."

**Q10.** What lesson does Metallgesellschaft teach about hedging?
> **A:** Economically sensible hedges can still kill a firm through funding liquidity — margin calls and rollover losses on short-dated futures before the long-dated exposure pays off.

**Q11.** Three types of foreign currency risk?
> **A:** Transaction risk (known future FX cash flows — hedge with forwards/options), translation risk (accounting consolidation of subsidiaries), and economic risk (long-run competitive exposure — managed structurally).

**Q12.** Forward/futures vs. options as hedging instruments?
> **A:** Forwards/futures are linear and symmetric, removing both upside and downside, no upfront cost. Options are asymmetric, keeping the favorable side for an upfront premium.

**Q13.** Exchange-traded vs. OTC derivatives?
> **A:** Exchange-traded: standardized, margined, low counterparty risk, but basis risk and margin liquidity demands. OTC: tailored but carry counterparty credit risk (mitigated by clearing/collateral).

**Q14.** What makes a risk limit framework effective?
> **A:** Clear ownership, monitoring, and escalation for breaches. A limit that is routinely waived is not a limit.
