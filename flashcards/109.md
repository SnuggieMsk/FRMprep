**Q1.** Savings & loan crisis — primary risk type and lesson?
> **A:** Interest rate risk. Thrifts funded long fixed-rate mortgages with short deposits; rates spiked → negative spread. Lesson: manage the asset-liability repricing gap; deposit insurance plus weak supervision breeds moral hazard.

**Q2.** Northern Rock (2007) — what kind of failure?
> **A:** Funding liquidity. ~75% wholesale/securitization-funded mortgage lender; markets froze → first UK bank run in 140 years. Lesson: liquidity risk can kill a solvent bank; diversify funding (LCR/NSFR rationale).

**Q3.** Lehman Brothers (2008) — the fatal combination?
> **A:** Funding liquidity / leverage. 30x+ leverage, illiquid real-estate assets, overnight repo funding; confidence loss → no rollover → bankruptcy. Lesson: leverage + illiquid assets + short funding is fatal.

**Q4.** Metallgesellschaft — exam trap classification?
> **A:** Hedging implementation / funding liquidity. The hedge was arguably sound economically; the firm died of margin calls and rollover losses on stack-and-roll short-dated futures — not speculation, not primarily basis risk.

**Q5.** Niederhoffer (1997) — the lesson?
> **A:** Model risk. Sold deep out-of-the-money S&P puts assuming no >5% daily crash; the Oct 1997 drop wiped the fund. Lesson: short-tail strategies look like alpha until the tail arrives.

**Q6.** LTCM (1998) — what did it demonstrate?
> **A:** Model risk / leverage / liquidity. ~25x leverage on convergence trades; Russia's default broke correlation and liquidity assumptions (correlations went to 1), triggering a margin spiral. Stress test beyond history; correlations rise in crises.

**Q7.** London Whale (JPMorgan CIO, 2012) — primary risk and lesson?
> **A:** Model risk / operational. A "hedge" ballooned into a prop trade; the VaR model was changed (spreadsheet errors) to halve reported risk; limit breaches ignored; ~$6bn loss. Lesson: model governance, independent validation, escalate breaches.

**Q8.** Barings (1995) — risk type and lesson?
> **A:** Rogue trading / operational. Leeson controlled both trading and back office in Singapore, hiding losses in account 88888. Lesson: segregate front and back office; investigate "too good" profits.

**Q9.** What do Barings, Sumitomo, and SocGen (Kerviel) share?
> **A:** Weak segregation of duties — traders used back-office knowledge/control gaps to conceal unauthorized positions. Fixes: enforce vacations/rotations, monitor cancelled/fake trades and gross (not just net) positions.

**Q10.** Bankers Trust (1994) — risk type and lesson?
> **A:** Financial engineering / conduct / reputation. Sold highly leveraged swaps to P&G and Gibson Greetings; staff boasted on tape about exploiting clients. Lesson: complex products sold over client comprehension = legal + reputational risk; suitability matters.

**Q11.** Orange County (1994) — what went wrong?
> **A:** Financial engineering / leverage. Treasurer Citron levered a "safe" fund ~3x via reverse repos into inverse floaters; 1994 rate hikes → ~$1.7bn loss, bankruptcy. Lesson: understand embedded leverage; reported yield ≠ low risk.

**Q12.** Enron (2001) and Volkswagen (2015) — risk types?
> **A:** Enron: corporate governance / accounting fraud (SPE-hidden debt, mark-to-market abuse; Arthur Andersen failed → Sarbanes-Oxley). VW: reputational risk from a conduct failure (emissions "defeat device").

**Q13.** SWIFT / Bangladesh Bank (2016) — risk type and lesson?
> **A:** Cyber risk. Hackers used stolen SWIFT credentials to order $951m of transfers; $81m got through. Lesson: payment authentication, reconciliation, and cyber controls are core operational risk — people/processes, not just firewalls.

**Q14.** Name the recurring failure patterns across the disasters.
> **A:** Leverage + illiquidity + short funding; concentration in one bet; control failures around individuals; model worship; governance/culture rot; and "hedges that aren't" (maturity mismatch, basis, mission creep).
