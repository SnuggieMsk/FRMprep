**Q1.** What is enterprise risk management (ERM)?
> **A:** A comprehensive, integrated, firm-wide approach in which all material risks are managed together as one portfolio against a single board-approved risk appetite, with risk tied to strategy and capital.

**Q2.** ERM vs. silo approach — the key distinction?
> **A:** Silos manage each risk type separately and miss cross-silo correlations/concentrations. ERM views all risks as one portfolio under a single firm-wide appetite, capturing interactions.

**Q3.** Exam trap: is ERM just "more risk measurement"?
> **A:** No. It is an organizational and governance change — one appetite, one aggregated view, one accountable executive (CRO), and risk embedded in strategic decisions.

**Q4.** What is the hedging-cost argument for ERM?
> **A:** Silos each hedge separately and ignore natural offsets, causing over-hedging. ERM nets natural offsets first and hedges only the net exposure — cheaper.

**Q5.** Name motivations for adopting ERM.
> **A:** Capture risk interactions/concentrations, hedging efficiency, better capital allocation (one economic-capital framework), earnings stability/lower distress, regulatory and rating-agency pressure, and strategic advantage.

**Q6.** Name the main costs/obstacles to ERM.
> **A:** Expensive data and systems integration, turf resistance, hard-to-quantify risks forced into one framework, and false comfort if the aggregate model is wrong.

**Q7.** Name ERM implementation best practices.
> **A:** Board ownership of appetite, a CRO with stature and independent reporting, appetite cascaded into limits, common risk taxonomy, integrated data/aggregation, risk in decision-making, and continuous improvement.

**Q8.** What is risk culture?
> **A:** The shared attitudes, values, and behaviors that determine how people actually identify, discuss, and act on risk — what employees do when no one is watching.

**Q9.** Markers of a strong risk culture (per FSB)?
> **A:** Tone from the top, accountability (breaches have consequences), effective communication and challenge (bad news travels up; dissent is safe), and incentives that reward risk-adjusted performance and escalation.

**Q10.** Exam trap: can a strong ERM framework coexist with failure?
> **A:** Yes. A firm can have a state-of-the-art ERM framework and still fail through weak risk culture (Barings, SocGen, London Whale all had limit frameworks). Culture questions are answered by behavior, not documents.

**Q11.** What is scenario analysis in ERM?
> **A:** Evaluating the firm-wide impact of specified adverse states (historical or hypothetical/forward-looking) by moving many risk factors coherently at once.

**Q12.** Advantages of scenario analysis over VaR?
> **A:** Captures tail events and interactions VaR misses, needs no probability estimates, is forward-looking (can include never-observed events), and reveals concentrations and contagion channels across silos.

**Q13.** Disadvantages of scenario analysis?
> **A:** Scenario choice is subjective ("failure of imagination"), no likelihoods attached, hypothetical scenarios may be inconsistent, and results are resource-intensive and sensitive to modeling assumptions.

**Q14.** What is reverse stress testing?
> **A:** Asking "what scenario breaks us?" — starting from firm failure and working back to the conditions that cause it, feeding the capital buffer above model-based economic capital.
