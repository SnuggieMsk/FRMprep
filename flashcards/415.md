**Q1.** What is the lognormal property of stock prices?
> **A:** Under GBM the price is lognormal and the continuously compounded return is normal: $$\ln S_T \sim N\!\left(\ln S_0 + (\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2})T,\ \sigma^2 T\right)$$. Prices can't go negative; the distribution is right-skewed.

**Q2.** Mean of the continuously compounded return, and why it's below μ.
> **A:** $$\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$$. The σ²/2 drag reflects that volatility erodes compound growth, so the expected cc return is less than the simple-return growth rate μ.

**Q3.** Compute the mean cc return: μ = 12%, σ = 30%.
> **A:** $$0.12 - \frac{0.09}{2} = 0.12 - 0.045 = 7.5\%$$.

**Q4.** Realized vs implied volatility.
> **A:** Realized: std dev of log returns, annualized ($$\hat\sigma = s\sqrt{252}$$ daily). Implied: the σ making the BSM price equal the market price, found by iteration — forward-looking (plus risk premia).

**Q5.** State the BSM call and put formulas.
> **A:** $$c = S_0 N(d_1) - Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$$; $$p = Ke^{-rT}N(-d_2) - S_0 N(-d_1)$$. They satisfy put-call parity $$c + Ke^{-rT} = p + S_0$$.

**Q6.** Write d₁ and d₂.
> **A:** $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0/K) + (r + \sigma^2/2)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}$$, $$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$$.

**Q7.** Interpret N(d₂) and N(d₁).
> **A:** N(d₂) = risk-neutral probability the call finishes in the money. N(d₁) = the call's delta (not the exercise probability).

**Q8.** Compute d₁: S₀ = 100, K = 95, r = 5%, σ = 20%, T = 0.5.
> **A:** $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(100/95) + (0.05+0.02)(0.5)}{0.20\sqrt{0.5}} = \frac{0.05129 + 0.035}{0.14142} = 0.6102$$; $$d_2 = 0.4688$$.

**Q9.** Two highest-frequency BSM slips.
> **A:** Using $$r - \sigma^2/2$$ instead of $$r + \sigma^2/2$$ in d₁ (the minus belongs to the lognormal mean of ln S_T), and forgetting to discount K (using K instead of $$Ke^{-rT}$$).

**Q10.** Adjust BSM for dividends.
> **A:** Discrete: replace S₀ with $$S_0 - PV(\text{divs})$$. Continuous yield q: replace S₀ with $$S_0 e^{-qT}$$ and use $$r-q$$ in d₁. Dividends lower call values and raise put values.

**Q11.** Find a put by put-call parity: c = 4.20, K = 50, S₀ = 50, r = 4%, T = 1.
> **A:** $$p = c + Ke^{-rT} - S_0 = 4.20 + 50e^{-0.04} - 50 = 4.20 + 48.039 - 50 = \$2.24$$.

**Q12.** When can American options be valued with BSM, and what is Black's approximation?
> **A:** American call on a non-dividend stock = European call (never exercised early), so BSM applies. With dividends, a call may be exercised just before an ex-dividend date; Black's approximation takes the larger of two European call values. American puts may be exercised early even without dividends — use a tree.

**Q13.** How does warrant dilution affect value?
> **A:** Exercise creates new shares and brings in cash, diluting holders: $$\text{Warrant value} \approx \frac{N}{N+M}\times c$$ (N shares, M warrants). The market typically impounds dilution at announcement.
