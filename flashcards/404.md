**Q1.** What is the investment-grade / high-yield boundary on each scale?
> **A:** Baa3 (Moody's) / BBB− (S&P, Fitch). A downgrade across it creates a "fallen angel" and can trigger forced selling.

**Q2.** What do agency ratings primarily measure?
> **A:** Default probability (S&P) or expected loss = PD × LGD (Moody's, especially structured products). Ratings are ordinal rankings of relative creditworthiness, not cardinal PDs.

**Q3.** Distinguish outlooks from watchlists.
> **A:** Outlook = positive/stable/negative, 1–2 year directional view. Watchlist = likely rating action within ~90 days. Neither changes the rating itself.

**Q4.** Contrast through-the-cycle (TTC) and point-in-time (PIT) rating philosophies.
> **A:** TTC (agencies): conditions averaged over a full cycle, stable, low procyclicality, lower short-term accuracy. PIT (many bank models): current conditions, volatile, higher short-term accuracy, high procyclicality.

**Q5.** What is a rating transition (migration) matrix?
> **A:** A matrix of probabilities of migrating between ratings over a period (rows = start, columns = end; rows sum to 100% including a default column). Properties: heavy diagonal (sticky), PD rises as rating falls, empirical rating momentum.

**Q6.** Why are multi-year default probabilities not just annual PD × years?
> **A:** You must walk the matrix, conditioning on each surviving state. Two-year PD = year-1 default + sum over each year-1 destination state of (transition prob × that state's year-2 default prob).

**Q7.** A BB issuer: stay BB 80%, to BBB 8%, to B 10%, default 2%. B defaults 6%, BBB defaults 0.5%. Two-year cumulative PD?
> **A:** $$0.02 + 0.80(0.02) + 0.10(0.06) + 0.08(0.005) = 0.02+0.016+0.006+0.0004 = 4.24\%$$.

**Q8.** Compute the marginal (conditional) year-2 PD given cumulative PDs of 5% (yr 1) and 11% (yr 2).
> **A:** $$PD_{2|1} = \frac{0.11-0.05}{1-0.05} = \frac{0.06}{0.95} = 6.32\%$$.

**Q9.** How do recovery rates relate to default rates?
> **A:** Negatively correlated — both driven by the cycle. Recoveries fall exactly when defaults spike, worsening tail losses. Recovery also depends on seniority/collateral (senior secured loans recover most).

**Q10.** How do internal bank ratings differ from agency ratings?
> **A:** Cover unrated borrowers (SMEs, retail); often two-dimensional (obligor PD rating + facility LGD/EAD rating); feed Basel IRB capital; must be validated, replicable, and independent of sales (rating-inflation risk).

**Q11.** Altman's Z-score idea and the public-manufacturer form.
> **A:** Discriminant function of accounting ratios mapped to a default signal: $$Z = 1.2X_1 + 1.4X_2 + 3.3X_3 + 0.6X_4 + 1.0X_5$$. Zones: Z > 3.0 safe, 1.8–3.0 gray, < 1.8 distress.

**Q12.** What conflict of interest plagues rating agencies?
> **A:** The issuer-pays model creates pressure to rate generously; acute in pre-2008 structured finance (AAA CDO tranches with flawed correlation assumptions, "rating shopping").

**Q13.** Why do high-rated and low-rated issuers' marginal PDs trend differently over time?
> **A:** Low-rated issuers' marginal PDs typically decline (survivors improve); high-rated issuers' marginal PDs rise (nowhere to go but down).
