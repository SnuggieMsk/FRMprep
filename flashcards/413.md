**Q1.** Why is a single factor (DV01/duration) not enough for hedging?
> **A:** A DV01-neutral position can still lose money: it assumes all rates move in lockstep, hides offsetting exposures large at individual maturities, and gives no guidance on which instruments to hedge with. Steepeners/flatteners/twists produce P&L despite "zero duration."

**Q2.** What are key rates and how is each key-rate shift shaped?
> **A:** A small set of par yields (commonly 2-, 5-, 10-, 30-year). Each shift moves its own rate 1bp, tapering linearly to zero at adjacent key rates (tent-shaped), flat beyond the end key rates. The shifts sum to a parallel 1bp shift.

**Q3.** Define KR01 and key-rate duration.
> **A:** $$\text{KR01}_k = -\frac{\Delta P_k}{10{,}000\cdot\Delta y_k}$$ and $$D_k = -\frac{1}{P}\frac{\Delta P_k}{\Delta y_k}$$, where $$\Delta P_k$$ is the repricing impact of shift k alone.

**Q4.** How do key-rate durations relate to effective duration?
> **A:** Because the shifts sum to a parallel shift, $$\sum_k D_k \approx D_{Eff}$$ and $$\sum_k \text{KR01}_k \approx \text{DV01}$$.

**Q5.** Key-rate durations are 0.8, 1.5, 3.2, 1.5. Effective duration?
> **A:** 0.8 + 1.5 + 3.2 + 1.5 = 7.0 (they sum to the parallel-shift duration).

**Q6.** Can a key-rate duration be negative?
> **A:** Yes — it is a curve-fitting artifact (e.g., a long zero discounted off a par-key-rate curve can gain when an intermediate key rate rises). GARP has tested this; don't panic.

**Q7.** How many instruments are needed to hedge n key-rate exposures?
> **A:** At least n hedging instruments, each concentrated near one key rate. Set hedge face amounts so total KR01 at every key rate is zero — a system of n linear equations (usually triangular: solve the single-unknown equation first).

**Q8.** Compute the price change from key-rate moves given a KR01 table.
> **A:** A dot product: $$\Delta P = -\sum_k \text{KR01}_k\times\Delta y_k^{(bp)}$$. Watch signs — one rate may fall while another rises.

**Q9.** A portfolio has a 10-year KR01 of +$2,400; hedge bond X has 10-year KR01 $60/$1M face. The hedge?
> **A:** Need offsetting −$2,400: 2,400 / 60 = 40 → short $40M face of X. Buying would double the exposure.

**Q10.** What are partial '01s?
> **A:** Shift one curve-fitting instrument's rate (e.g., each par swap rate) by 1bp, refit, reprice — one '01 per curve input (10–20 buckets). Used by swap desks. Partial '01s sum to the total '01.

**Q11.** What are forward-bucket '01s?
> **A:** Shift all forward rates within a maturity bucket (e.g., 0–2, 2–5, 5–10, 10–30 years) by 1bp and reprice. Natural for forward-priced instruments; bucket '01s sum to the total.

**Q12.** What is volatility-weighted (regression-based) hedging?
> **A:** $$N = -\frac{\text{DV01}_{position}}{\text{DV01}_{hedge}}\times\beta$$, where β is the regression slope (change in position yield per unit change in hedge yield). Minimizes P&L variance but introduces model risk (β drifts).

**Q13.** Why does a DV01-neutral long-10y / short-2s30s-barbell lose money?
> **A:** It is flat to parallel moves by construction but short convexity/curvature — it loses when the wings (2y, 30y) rally relative to the belly (10y), i.e., on a curvature change.
