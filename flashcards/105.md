**Q1.** What is the core insight of modern portfolio theory (Markowitz)?
> **A:** Investors care only about the mean and variance of portfolio returns, and diversification reduces risk because assets are imperfectly correlated.

**Q2.** Formula for two-asset portfolio variance?
> **A:** $$\sigma_p^2 = w_1^2\sigma_1^2 + w_2^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w_1 w_2 \rho_{12}\sigma_1\sigma_2$$ with $$\mathrm{Cov}(R_1,R_2)=\rho_{12}\sigma_1\sigma_2$$.

**Q3.** When does portfolio standard deviation equal the weighted average of individual volatilities?
> **A:** Only when $$\rho = +1$$. For any $$\rho < 1$$, portfolio volatility is strictly less — the diversification benefit.

**Q4.** What is the efficient frontier?
> **A:** The set of portfolios with the highest expected return for each level of risk — the upper edge of the feasible set from the global minimum-variance portfolio upward.

**Q5.** What is the two-fund separation theorem?
> **A:** With a risk-free asset and homogeneous expectations, every investor holds the same risky (market) portfolio and adjusts risk only via the weight in the risk-free asset.

**Q6.** Formula for beta?
> **A:** $$\beta_i = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(R_i, R_m)}{\sigma_m^2} = \rho_{im}\,\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_m}$$ — sensitivity to systematic (market) risk.

**Q7.** The CAPM expected-return formula?
> **A:** $$E[R_i] = R_f + \beta_i\left(E[R_m] - R_f\right)$$ — only systematic risk is rewarded; idiosyncratic risk is diversifiable and earns no premium.

**Q8.** How is portfolio beta computed?
> **A:** As the weighted average of component betas: $$\beta_p = \sum_i w_i\beta_i$$.

**Q9.** Name the CAPM assumptions.
> **A:** Rational mean–variance optimizers over one common period; no taxes/transaction costs, divisible assets; unlimited borrowing/lending at $$R_f$$; homogeneous expectations; price-taking investors with free information.

**Q10.** CML vs. SML — risk axis and applicability?
> **A:** CML uses total risk $$\sigma_p$$ and applies only to efficient portfolios (mixes of $$R_f$$ and the market). SML uses systematic risk $$\beta_i$$ and applies to all assets and portfolios.

**Q11.** Equations for the CML and SML?
> **A:** CML: $$E[R_p] = R_f + \frac{E[R_m]-R_f}{\sigma_m}\,\sigma_p$$ (slope = market Sharpe). SML: $$E[R_i] = R_f + \beta_i(E[R_m]-R_f)$$ (slope = market risk premium).

**Q12.** What does it mean to plot above the SML?
> **A:** The asset is underpriced — its expected return exceeds the CAPM-required return (positive alpha). Below the SML means overpriced.

**Q13.** Exam trap: a fairly priced single stock with large idiosyncratic risk plots where?
> **A:** On the SML but below the CML. Only efficient portfolios sit on the CML. Use beta (not total σ) in the SML; using σ for an individual stock's CAPM return is the classic wrong answer.

**Q14.** What is Roll's critique of the CAPM?
> **A:** The true market portfolio (all risky assets, including human capital and real estate) is unobservable, so the CAPM is not properly testable.
