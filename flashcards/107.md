**Q1.** Sharpe ratio formula and what risk it uses?
> **A:** $$S_p = \frac{E[R_p] - R_f}{\sigma_p}$$ — excess return per unit of total risk (σ).

**Q2.** Treynor ratio formula and what risk it uses?
> **A:** $$T_p = \frac{E[R_p] - R_f}{\beta_p}$$ — excess return per unit of systematic risk (beta).

**Q3.** Jensen's alpha formula?
> **A:** $$\alpha_p = E[R_p] - \left[R_f + \beta_p(E[R_m] - R_f)\right]$$ — return above the CAPM-required return; an absolute measure, not a ratio.

**Q4.** Most-tested trap: Sharpe vs. Treynor denominator?
> **A:** Sharpe divides by total risk (σ); Treynor divides by beta. A poorly diversified fund can look fine on Treynor yet terrible on Sharpe because idiosyncratic risk inflates σ but not β.

**Q5.** When use Sharpe vs. Treynor/alpha?
> **A:** Use Sharpe when the fund is the investor's entire wealth (or undiversified). Use Treynor/alpha when it is one of many funds held within a diversified whole (only systematic risk matters).

**Q6.** Tracking error and information ratio?
> **A:** $$TE = \sigma(R_p - R_b)$$ (volatility of active returns). $$IR = \frac{E[R_p] - E[R_b]}{TE}$$ — average active return per unit of tracking error; used to judge active managers vs. benchmark.

**Q7.** What is the Sortino ratio and when is it preferred?
> **A:** $$\text{Sortino} = \frac{E[R_p] - R_{min}}{\sigma_{down}}$$ — penalizes only downside deviation below a minimum acceptable return. Preferred for asymmetric returns (options strategies, hedge funds).

**Q8.** RAROC definition?
> **A:** $$RAROC = \frac{\text{expected after-tax risk-adjusted net income}}{\text{economic capital}}$$ — charges each business for the economic capital its risk consumes.

**Q9.** In the RAROC numerator, how are expected losses treated?
> **A:** Deducted as a cost of doing business (they are priced, not capitalized). Economic capital covers unexpected losses, not EL.

**Q10.** Why is "return on economic capital" added back in RAROC?
> **A:** The economic capital buffer is invested in safe assets earning roughly the risk-free rate, so that income is added to the numerator.

**Q11.** RAROC decision rule?
> **A:** Accept activities whose RAROC exceeds the hurdle rate — the firm's cost of the capital backing the business (commonly the weighted cost of equity capital).

**Q12.** What problem does ARAROC fix, and what is its formula?
> **A:** Plain RAROC ignores the systematic riskiness of returns. $$ARAROC = RAROC - \beta_E(E[R_m] - R_f)$$.

**Q13.** ARAROC decision rule?
> **A:** Accept the project if $$ARAROC > R_f$$ (equivalently, if RAROC exceeds the CAPM-required return $$R_f + \beta_E(E[R_m]-R_f)$$).

**Q14.** Name key challenges/limitations of risk-adjusted performance measures.
> **A:** Economic capital and beta are model outputs; allocation of diversification benefit changes every unit's RAROC; horizon mismatches; backward-looking inputs; ratios mislead for non-normal returns (e.g., short-options Sharpe).
