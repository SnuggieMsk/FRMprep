**Q1.** What does "linear" mean in linear regression, and give an estimable vs. non-estimable example.
> **A:** Linear in the PARAMETERS. $$Y = \alpha + \beta X^2 + \epsilon$$ and $$\ln Y = \alpha + \beta\ln X + \epsilon$$ are estimable (transform the data); $$Y = \alpha + X^\beta + \epsilon$$ is not.

**Q2.** Give the OLS slope estimator in its three equivalent forms.
> **A:** $$\hat\beta = \dfrac{\sum (X_i - \bar X)(Y_i - \bar Y)}{\sum (X_i - \bar X)^2} = \dfrac{\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(X,Y)}{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(X)} = \rho_{XY}\dfrac{s_Y}{s_X}$$.

**Q3.** Give the OLS intercept and two in-sample properties of the fitted line.
> **A:** $$\hat\alpha = \bar Y - \hat\beta\bar X$$. The line passes through $$(\bar X, \bar Y)$$; residuals sum to zero and are uncorrelated with $$X$$.

**Q4.** List the key OLS assumptions.
> **A:** (1) $$E[\epsilon \mid X] = 0$$ (exogeneity); (2) $$(X_i, Y_i)$$ iid; (3) positive variance of $$X$$, finite fourth moments; (4) homoskedasticity $$\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon \mid X) = \sigma^2$$ and no error correlation.

**Q5.** Which assumption violation biases the coefficient, and which only corrupts the standard errors?
> **A:** Violating exogeneity (assumption 1) biases $$\hat\beta$$ — the deadly one. Violating homoskedasticity does NOT bias $$\hat\beta$$; it invalidates the standard errors and t-tests.

**Q6.** Give the total-variation decomposition and the formula for $$R^2$$.
> **A:** $$TSS = ESS + RSS$$; $$R^2 = \dfrac{ESS}{TSS} = 1 - \dfrac{RSS}{TSS}$$. In single-variable regression $$R^2 = \rho_{XY}^2$$.

**Q7.** Give the standard error of the regression.
> **A:** $$s_\epsilon = \sqrt{RSS/(n-2)}$$ — two parameters estimated.

**Q8.** Give the t-statistic and CI for a single regression coefficient.
> **A:** $$t = \dfrac{\hat\beta - \beta_0}{SE(\hat\beta)} \sim t_{n-2}$$; CI $$= \hat\beta \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-2}\,SE(\hat\beta)$$.

**Q9.** $$\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(R_s, R_m) = 0.0024$$, $$\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(R_m) = 0.0016$$. Find beta.
> **A:** $$\hat\beta = 0.0024/0.0016 = 1.50$$ (this is exactly the CAPM beta estimator).

**Q10.** $$s_X = 4\%$$, $$s_Y = 6\%$$, $$\rho_{XY} = 0.5$$. Find the OLS slope of $$Y$$ on $$X$$.
> **A:** $$\hat\beta = \rho\dfrac{s_Y}{s_X} = 0.5 \times 6/4 = 0.75$$.

**Q11.** $$\hat\beta = 0.85$$, $$SE = 0.30$$, $$n = 62$$ (≈60 d.o.f., crit 2.0). Test $$H_0:\beta = 0$$ and $$H_0:\beta = 1$$.
> **A:** $$\beta=0$$: $$t = 0.85/0.30 = 2.83 > 2.0$$ → reject (significant). $$\beta=1$$: $$t = (0.85-1)/0.30 = -0.50$$ → fail to reject (indistinguishable from a one-for-one hedge).

**Q12.** In single-variable OLS, $$R^2 = 0.49$$. What is the correlation between $$X$$ and $$Y$$?
> **A:** $$\rho = \pm\sqrt{0.49} = \pm 0.70$$; the sign matches the slope's sign.

**Q13.** Interpret $$\hat\beta$$ and $$\hat\alpha$$.
> **A:** $$\hat\beta$$ = expected change in $$Y$$ per one-unit change in $$X$$; $$\hat\alpha$$ = expected $$Y$$ when $$X = 0$$.
