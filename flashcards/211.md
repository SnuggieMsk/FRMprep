**Q1.** Give the linear and log-linear time-trend models and what each captures.
> **A:** Linear: $$Y_t = \delta_0 + \delta_1 t + \epsilon_t$$ (mean changes with time). Log-linear: $$\ln Y_t = \delta_0 + \delta_1 t + \epsilon_t$$ models constant percentage growth $$\delta_1$$ per period.

**Q2.** How do you make a trend-stationary series stationary?
> **A:** Detrend it — subtract the fitted trend (regress on $$t$$); shocks then die out and the series returns to the trend line.

**Q3.** Define a unit root and the canonical random walk.
> **A:** A unit root means the AR lag polynomial has a root equal to one ($$\phi = 1$$). Random walk: $$Y_t = Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t = Y_0 + \sum_{i=1}^t \epsilon_i$$.

**Q4.** Give the mean and variance of a driftless random walk.
> **A:** $$E[Y_t] = Y_0$$; $$\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = t\sigma^2$$ — variance grows without bound, shocks are permanent, best forecast at any horizon is the current value.

**Q5.** Give the random walk with drift and its moments.
> **A:** $$Y_t = \delta + Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$; $$E[Y_t] = Y_0 + \delta t$$ and variance is still $$t\sigma^2$$.

**Q6.** Distinguish trend-stationary from difference-stationary, and the right fix for each.
> **A:** Trend-stationary reverts to its trend after a shock — detrend it. Unit-root (difference-stationary) never reverts — difference it. Using the wrong fix produces misspecification.

**Q7.** What is spurious regression and its telltale sign?
> **A:** Regressing one independent random walk on another yields large $$R^2$$ and significant t-stats despite zero true relationship. Telltale: high $$R^2$$ with strongly autocorrelated residuals (very low Durbin–Watson). Work with returns/differences instead.

**Q8.** Write the Dickey–Fuller regression and its hypotheses.
> **A:** $$\Delta Y_t = \delta + \gamma Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$ with $$\gamma = \phi - 1$$. $$H_0: \gamma = 0$$ (unit root) vs. $$H_1: \gamma < 0$$ (stationary) — a one-sided, lower-tail test.

**Q9.** Why can't you use ordinary t critical values in the Dickey–Fuller test?
> **A:** Under $$H_0$$ the statistic does not follow a t-distribution; it follows the Dickey–Fuller distribution with more-negative critical values (about $$-2.86$$ at 5% with a constant vs. $$-1.645$$ for a normal one-tailed test).

**Q10.** What does the augmented Dickey–Fuller (ADF) test add, and what is its power limitation?
> **A:** ADF adds lagged $$\Delta Y$$ terms to soak up serial correlation. It has low power against near-unit-root alternatives ($$\phi = 0.98$$), so failing to reject does not prove a unit root.

**Q11.** Driftless random walk, daily $$\sigma = \$2$$, today's price $150. Find the 30-day forecast and SD.
> **A:** $$\hat Y_{t+30} = 150$$; $$\mathrm{Var} = 30(4) = 120$$, $$\sigma_{30} = \sqrt{120} = 10.95$$.

**Q12.** ADF on an index level ($$T=250$$): $$\hat\gamma = -0.020$$, $$SE = 0.011$$, crit $$\approx -2.86$$. Decision?
> **A:** $$DF = -0.020/0.011 = -1.82 > -2.86$$ (not negative enough) → fail to reject the unit root; treat as a random walk and model returns.

**Q13.** List the steps to build a model from a non-stationary series.
> **A:** Plot and identify trend/seasonality/random walk → detrend or add seasonal dummies → ADF test; if unit root, first-difference (log differences for prices) → model the stationary series with ARMA → verify white-noise residuals (Ljung–Box).
