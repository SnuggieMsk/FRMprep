**Q1.** State the Basel definition of operational risk and its boundary.
> **A:** The risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems, or from external events. Includes legal risk; excludes strategic and reputational risk.

**Q2.** Name the seven Basel operational-risk event types.
> **A:** Internal fraud; external fraud; employment practices & workplace safety; clients, products & business practices; damage to physical assets; business disruption & system failures; execution, delivery & process management.

**Q3.** Contrast HFLS and LFHS operational losses.
> **A:** High-frequency/low-severity (processing/settlement errors) are budgetable, near EL. Low-frequency/high-severity (rogue trader, massive fines) drive the capital tail.

**Q4.** Which event type produced the largest dollar losses post-2008?
> **A:** Clients, products & business practices (type 4) — compliance/conduct failures: mis-selling, money-laundering fines, market manipulation (multi-billion fines).

**Q5.** How does the loss distribution approach (LDA) model losses?
> **A:** As a compound distribution: frequency (number of losses) × severity (size). Combined by Monte Carlo to get the aggregate annual loss distribution. Capital = 99.9% quantile (minus EL, per regulation).

**Q6.** What distributions are typically used for frequency and severity?
> **A:** Frequency: Poisson (intensity λ). Severity: lognormal (or extreme-value distributions for the tail). Expected annual loss = $$\lambda \times E[\text{severity}]$$.

**Q7.** Compute expected annual loss: λ = 12, mean severity $80,000.
> **A:** $$12 \times 80{,}000 = \$960{,}000$$ per year. Note: the 99.9% tail depends on severity's tail (lognormal σ), which dominates capital.

**Q8.** What were the four AMA inputs?
> **A:** Internal loss data, external data (consortium, e.g., ORX — needs scaling), scenario analysis (fills the tail), and BEICFs (business environment and internal control factors).

**Q9.** Name the Basel op-risk capital approaches and the current standard.
> **A:** Basic Indicator (BIA), Standardized (TSA), AMA (withdrawn — too much variance/gaming), and the current SMA (Standardized Measurement Approach, since 2023).

**Q10.** What is the SMA capital structure?
> **A:** $$\text{Capital} = BIC \times ILM$$. BIC = Business Indicator × marginal coefficients (~12/15/18% by size bucket). ILM scales capital by 10-year historical loss experience; ILM = 1 when loss component = BIC.

**Q11.** Distinguish RCSA, KRIs, and scenario analysis.
> **A:** RCSA = units self-assess risks and control effectiveness (subjective, self-serving bias). KRIs = forward-looking metrics (turnover, downtime, unreconciled trades) with escalation thresholds. Scenario analysis = expert workshops quantifying plausible LFHS events.

**Q12.** Does holding capital reduce operational risk?
> **A:** No — capital absorbs losses; controls reduce them. Trap: op risk is not lowered by holding more capital.

**Q13.** What moral hazard arises from allocating op-risk capital by loss history alone?
> **A:** Units hide losses to lower their capital. Allocation should reward good control environments so lower capital incentivizes better controls.
