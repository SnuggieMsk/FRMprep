**Q1.** When does the mean-variance framework give correct risk rankings?
> **A:** Only when returns are elliptically distributed (normal, finite-variance Student's t). It fails for skewed returns, fat tails, and non-linear (option) payoffs, where σ understates tail risk.

**Q2.** Define value at risk (VaR) at confidence level α.
> **A:** The loss not exceeded with probability α — the α-quantile of the loss distribution: $$P(L > \text{VaR}_\alpha) = 1 - \alpha$$. It is a quantile, not a worst case.

**Q3.** Parametric VaR formula for normal P&L.
> **A:** $$\text{VaR}_\alpha = -\mu + z_\alpha \sigma$$, with $$z_{0.95}=1.645$$, $$z_{0.99}=2.326$$. With μ = 0, VaR = $$z_\alpha\sigma$$.

**Q4.** Compute the 99% one-day VaR for a portfolio with σ = $200,000 and μ = 0.
> **A:** $$2.326 \times 200{,}000 = \$465{,}200$$.

**Q5.** What is the key limitation of VaR regarding the tail?
> **A:** It says nothing about losses beyond the quantile. Two books with identical VaR can have very different tail severity; VaR puts no ceiling on how bad a tail loss is.

**Q6.** List the four properties of a coherent risk measure.
> **A:** Monotonicity, subadditivity ($$\rho(X_1+X_2)\le\rho(X_1)+\rho(X_2)$$), positive homogeneity ($$\rho(\lambda X)=\lambda\rho(X)$$), and translation invariance ($$\rho(X+c)=\rho(X)-c$$).

**Q7.** Which coherence property does VaR violate, and when?
> **A:** Subadditivity — for non-elliptical distributions, merging portfolios can raise measured VaR, penalizing diversification. VaR is subadditive when returns are normal.

**Q8.** Define expected shortfall (ES) and its normal-distribution formula.
> **A:** $$\text{ES}_\alpha = E[L\mid L>\text{VaR}_\alpha]$$. For normal P&L: $$\text{ES}_\alpha = -\mu + \sigma\frac{\phi(z_\alpha)}{1-\alpha}$$, with $$\phi(1.645)=0.1031$$, $$\phi(2.326)=0.0267$$.

**Q9.** Why is ES coherent while VaR is not?
> **A:** ES averages all quantiles beyond α (giving larger weights to worse outcomes), satisfying all four coherence properties including subadditivity. It is the simplest coherent spectral risk measure.

**Q10.** Relationship between ES and VaR at the same confidence level.
> **A:** $$\text{ES}_\alpha \ge \text{VaR}_\alpha$$ always. VaR answers "how often?"; ES answers "how bad is bad?"

**Q11.** Compute the 95% ES for a portfolio with σ = $200,000, μ = 0.
> **A:** $$200{,}000 \times \frac{0.1031}{0.05} = 200{,}000 \times 2.0627 = \$412{,}540$$.

**Q12.** Two independent bonds each default 3% of the time with a $10M loss. Why does VaR fail subadditivity at 95%?
> **A:** Each alone: P(loss)=3% < 5%, so 95% VaR = $0; sum = $0. Together P(at least one default) = $$1-0.97^2=5.91\%>5\%$$, so VaR(A+B) = $10M > $0 = sum. Diversified portfolio shows more VaR.

**Q13.** Which regulatory regime uses ES, and at what level?
> **A:** FRTB uses 97.5% ES (≈ 99% VaR for normal: multiplier 2.338 vs 2.326). Basel II market risk used 99%, 10-day VaR.
