**Q1.** Day-count conventions by instrument
> **A:** US Treasury bonds/notes: Actual/Actual. US corporate and municipal bonds: 30/360. US money market (T-bills, SOFR): Actual/360. Accrued interest = period coupon × accrual fraction.

**Q2.** T-bill cash price from a quoted discount rate
> **A:** $$P = 100 - \frac{n}{360}\,Q$$ per $100 face, for discount $$Q$$ over $$n$$ days. The discount rate understates true yield because it divides by face (100), not price, and uses a 360-day year.

**Q3.** Clean vs. dirty price
> **A:** Bonds are quoted clean but you pay dirty: $$\text{Cash (dirty) price} = \text{Quoted (clean) price} + \text{Accrued interest}$$.

**Q4.** Cash received by the short in a T-bond futures delivery
> **A:** $$\text{Cash} = (\text{Futures settlement} \times \text{CF}) + \text{Accrued interest}$$, where CF is the conversion factor — the price of the delivered bond per $1 face at a flat 6% yield.

**Q5.** Cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) selection rule
> **A:** Short delivers the bond minimizing $$\text{Cost} = \text{Quoted bond price} - (\text{Futures price} \times \text{CF})$$. When yields exceed 6%, low-coupon long-maturity bonds tend to be CTD; below 6%, high-coupon short-maturity.

**Q6.** Wild card play and delivery options
> **A:** Futures settle at 2:00 p.m. but the short can give notice of delivery later that evening at the 2:00 price; if cash prices fall after the close, delivering cheap at the fixed invoice price profits. These embedded short options reduce the futures price.

**Q7.** Eurodollar / 3-month SOFR futures quote and value per basis point
> **A:** Quote $$= 100 - R$$. One basis point moves the contract by $$1{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0001 \times \frac{90}{360} = \$25$$. A long gains when the quote rises (rates fall).

**Q8.** Final contract price of a Eurodollar futures contract
> **A:** $$10{,}000 \times [100 - 0.25(100 - Q)]$$, where $$Q$$ is the quote. SOFR futures work the same but settle to compounded realized SOFR over the quarter.

**Q9.** Convexity adjustment: futures rate to forward rate
> **A:** $$\text{forward} = \text{futures} - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t_1 t_2$$. Daily settlement biases the futures rate upward, so the forward is lower. The adjustment grows with the square of volatility and (roughly) maturity — negligible short, large beyond a few years.

**Q10.** Why is the futures rate above the forward rate?
> **A:** Daily margining favors the short-rate position priced into futures: gains arrive when rates are high (reinvested well) and losses fund cheaply when rates are low. So the futures rate is biased upward versus the forward.

**Q11.** Number of contracts in a duration-based hedge
> **A:** $$N^* = -\frac{P\,D_P}{F_C\,D_F}$$, with $$P$$ = portfolio value, $$D_P$$ its duration, $$F_C$$ = futures contract price, $$D_F$$ = underlying duration. Negative $$N^*$$ means short futures.

**Q12.** Duration-hedge traps
> **A:** Assumes a small parallel yield-curve shift — fails for twists, large moves, and convexity. For T-bond futures use the duration of the CTD bond at the futures expiry for $$D_F$$, not the portfolio's.

**Q13.** Accrued interest for a Treasury (Actual/Actual)
> **A:** $$\text{AI} = \text{period coupon} \times \frac{\text{actual days since last coupon}}{\text{actual days in coupon period}}$$. For a corporate bond use 30/360 days instead — a small but testable difference.

**Q14.** CTD formula trap — clean vs. dirty
> **A:** The CTD cost subtracts $$\text{futures} \times \text{CF}$$ from the quoted (clean) price. Adding accrued interest to both legs cancels, so dirty prices are a distractor.
