**Q1.** Gross realized return over a holding period.
> **A:** $$R = \frac{P_{end} + \text{coupons} + \text{reinvestment income} - P_{start}}{P_{start}}$$. Net return subtracts financing cost (e.g., repo interest).

**Q2.** When does realized return equal the purchase YTM?
> **A:** Only if every coupon is reinvested at the YTM and the bond is held to maturity. Lower reinvestment rates drag realized return below YTM.

**Q3.** Define a bond's spread over a benchmark curve.
> **A:** The constant s added to every benchmark spot/forward rate so discounted cash flows match market price: $$P = \sum_t \frac{CF_t}{(1+(z_t+s)/2)^{2t}}$$. It isolates credit/liquidity/mispricing.

**Q4.** Define yield to maturity.
> **A:** The single discount rate equating PV of cash flows to price. It is a cash-flow-weighted "complex average" of spot rates and is bond-specific (coupon-dependent when the curve isn't flat).

**Q5.** Premium / par / discount conditions.
> **A:** Coupon rate > YTM → premium (P > 100). Coupon = YTM → par. Coupon < YTM → discount (P < 100).

**Q6.** What is "pull to par"?
> **A:** Holding yield constant, a premium bond's price drifts down to 100 and a discount bond's up to 100 as maturity approaches.

**Q7.** Price a 2-year 6% semiannual bond at 5% YTM.
> **A:** Period rate 2.5%, 4 periods, coupon 3. Annuity factor = $$\frac{1-1.025^{-4}}{0.025} = 3.762$$. $$P = 3(3.762) + 100(0.90595) = 11.29 + 90.60 = \$101.88$$ (premium).

**Q8.** Perpetuity price formula and example.
> **A:** $$P = \frac{C}{y}$$. A $4 perpetuity at 5% → $80; at 4% → $100 (a 25% gain illustrates perpetuities' extreme duration).

**Q9.** Annuity price formula.
> **A:** $$P = C\times\frac{1 - (1+y)^{-n}}{y}$$. Example: $4 for 10 years at 5% → $$4\times7.7217 = \$30.89$$.

**Q10.** Contrast price risk and reinvestment risk by horizon.
> **A:** Short horizon: price risk dominates (rates up → loss). Long horizon: reinvestment risk dominates (rates up → coupons compound faster → gain). They roughly offset at a horizon equal to duration (immunization).

**Q11.** Why is the zero-coupon bond the exception to the reinvestment assumption?
> **A:** No coupons → no reinvestment assumption → YTM held to maturity is exactly the realized return. YTM is otherwise a quoting convention, not a promised return.

**Q12.** Bond-equivalent vs effective annual yield for a 5% semiannual YTM.
> **A:** 5% semiannual = 2.5% per period; effective annual = $$(1.025)^2 - 1 = 5.06\%$$. Mixing the two is a recurring distractor.

**Q13.** Decompose bond P&L; which components remain if the curve is unchanged?
> **A:** Return = coupon income (carry) + rolldown + curve-change impact + spread change. If the curve is unchanged, only carry + rolldown remain.
