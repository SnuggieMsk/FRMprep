**Q1.** What is novation in central clearing?
> **A:** When a trade is accepted for clearing, novation replaces the original bilateral contract with two contracts — the CCP becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer. Members then face only the CCP's credit, not each other's.

**Q2.** Why does a CCP have no market risk?
> **A:** It runs a matched book — every long is offset by a short. Its exposure is purely counterparty risk: if a member defaults, the book is no longer matched.

**Q3.** Multilateral netting: A owes B $20M, B owes C $30M, C owes A $40M. Net positions vs the CCP?
> **A:** A receives $20M (pays 20, receives 40); B pays $10M (pays 30, receives 20); C pays $10M (pays 40, receives 30). Total flows fall from $90M gross to $40M.

**Q4.** Name the three layers of the CCP margin stack.
> **A:** Variation margin (daily mark-to-market settlement, paid by the loser), initial margin (per-portfolio buffer covering close-out losses over the margin period of risk at high confidence), and default fund contributions (mutualized layer sized to extreme but plausible scenarios — Cover 1/Cover 2).

**Q5.** What is the exact order of the CCP default waterfall?
> **A:** 1) Defaulter's initial margin; 2) defaulter's default fund contribution; 3) CCP's own capital (skin in the game); 4) surviving members' default fund contributions; 5) further assessments / recovery tools (cash calls, VM gains haircutting, partial tear-up).

**Q6.** Exam trap: can a non-defaulting member's initial margin absorb another member's losses?
> **A:** No. A non-defaulter's IM is never mutualized — it is returned if that member never defaults. Only survivors' default fund contributions and assessment rights are mutualized. IM sits at the top of the waterfall only for the defaulter.

**Q7.** What is porting in CCP default management?
> **A:** Transferring a defaulted member's client positions, with their collateral, to a surviving clearing member. It works best with segregated client margin and a willing backup member.

**Q8.** How does a CCP manage a member default?
> **A:** Declare default and halt trading, port client positions, hedge the defaulter's house book to neutralize market risk, auction the book to surviving members (default fund contributions can be juniorized against poor bids), then run any losses through the waterfall.

**Q9.** Give three advantages of central clearing.
> **A:** Counterparty risk reduction (novation, two-way daily margining, multilateral netting, mutualized resources); transparency (hub-and-spoke replaces bilateral webs); liquidity/standardization with orderly default management (e.g., Lehman's cleared book wound down without CCP loss).

**Q10.** What is procyclicality of CCP initial margin?
> **A:** IM models raise requirements when volatility spikes, draining members' liquidity exactly when funding is scarce in stressed markets — amplifying the stress.

**Q11.** List key disadvantages/risks of central clearing.
> **A:** Concentration of risk (CCP is a systemically critical single point of failure, "too big to fail"); procyclical margins; moral hazard (less counterparty monitoring; CCP race to the bottom); model risk in the tails; CCP-specific default, investment, liquidity, operational, and legal risks; bespoke trades stay bilateral.

**Q12.** Contrast the principal-to-principal and agency client clearing models.
> **A:** Principal-to-principal (European): client faces the clearing member, who faces the CCP with a back-to-back mirror trade — the member is principal to both. Agency (US FCM): the client's contract is with the CCP; the member acts as agent and guarantees the client's performance.

**Q13.** Does multilateral netting always increase netting benefits?
> **A:** No. It helps most when exposures are well distributed, but it can reduce netting benefits if it splits products across multiple CCPs that were previously netted bilaterally under a single ISDA agreement.

**Q14.** Why is CCP margining historically stricter than bilateral CSA collateral?
> **A:** CCP margin is two-way, zero-threshold, marked daily, and includes initial margin. Post-crisis rules now impose IM and VM on non-cleared bilateral trades too, narrowing the gap.
