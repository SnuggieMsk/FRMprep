**Q1.** Define delta for BSM calls and puts (no dividends).
> **A:** $$\delta_{call} = N(d_1) \in (0,1)$$; $$\delta_{put} = N(d_1) - 1 \in (-1,0)$$. ATM call delta is near 0.5; portfolio delta = position-weighted sum, with one share = delta 1.

**Q2.** What is dynamic delta hedging?
> **A:** A short call is hedged by holding $$N(d_1)$$ shares per option; because delta changes with S and t, the hedge must be rebalanced. The rebalancing cost (buy high, sell low for a short-gamma hedger) is what the premium pays for.

**Q3.** Delta-hedge 1,000 written calls with delta 0.62.
> **A:** Position delta = −1,000 × 0.62 = −620 → buy 620 shares to reach zero delta.

**Q4.** Deltas of linear instruments.
> **A:** Share = +1; forward on a non-dividend stock = exactly 1; futures contract = $$e^{rT}$$ (daily settlement) — so hedging with futures needs slightly fewer contracts.

**Q5.** Define theta and its usual sign.
> **A:** $$\Theta = \partial f/\partial t$$ (time decay), usually quoted per day. Negative for most long options; most negative for ATM options near expiry. Exception: a deep ITM European put can have positive theta.

**Q6.** Define gamma, its sign, and where it peaks.
> **A:** $$\Gamma = \partial^2 f/\partial S^2 = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S_0\sigma\sqrt{T}}$$. Positive for long calls and puts; largest for ATM options near expiry. It measures how fast a delta hedge goes stale.

**Q7.** Delta-gamma approximation for a position's value change.
> **A:** $$\Delta P \approx \delta\,\Delta S + \frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^2$$ — the option twin of duration-plus-convexity. A delta-neutral long-gamma book makes ≈ ½Γ(ΔS)² on any large move.

**Q8.** A delta-neutral book has gamma +8,000; the stock jumps $3. Approximate P&L?
> **A:** $$\frac{1}{2}(8{,}000)(3)^2 = +\$36{,}000$$, regardless of direction — the payoff of long gamma.

**Q9.** Vega and rho: signs and where they peak.
> **A:** Vega = ∂f/∂σ, positive for long calls and puts, highest for ATM and longer maturities. Rho = ∂f/∂r, positive for calls, negative for puts; larger for ITM/long-dated options.

**Q10.** Delta-hedge 500 written puts with delta −0.38.
> **A:** Position delta = −500 × (−0.38) = +190 → sell 190 shares. (Buying doubles the exposure.)

**Q11.** How do you build a delta-gamma-neutral portfolio, and why does order matter?
> **A:** Step 1: add $$w = -\Gamma_{port}/\Gamma_{option}$$ options to zero gamma. Step 2: re-neutralize delta with shares. Hedge gamma before delta — shares have zero gamma, so step 2 doesn't undo step 1, but fixing delta first gets wrecked by the options added for gamma.

**Q12.** State the theta-gamma trade-off for a delta-neutral portfolio.
> **A:** From the BSM PDE, $$\Theta + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \Gamma = r\Pi$$: large positive gamma forces large negative theta. You can't own convexity without paying time decay — theta is the rent on gamma.

**Q13.** Which Greeks are identical for a European call and put with the same strike/expiry?
> **A:** Gamma and vega (the S₀ and K terms drop out of put-call parity). Delta, theta, and rho all differ.

**Q14.** What is portfolio insurance and its key limitation?
> **A:** Synthetic protective put: sell stock/index futures as the market falls, buy as it rises. It demands liquidity exactly when markets gap (it amplified the 1987 crash); it assumes continuous trading and stable volatility — the conditions that fail in a crash.
