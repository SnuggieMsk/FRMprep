**Q1.** Distinguish an estimator from an estimate.
> **A:** An estimator is the formula (a random variable); an estimate is the number it produces from a particular sample.

**Q2.** Give the unbiased sample variance formula and explain the divisor.
> **A:** $$s^2 = \dfrac{1}{n-1}\sum (X_i - \bar X)^2$$. The $$n-1$$ divisor (one d.o.f. spent estimating $$\bar X$$) makes $$s^2$$ unbiased; dividing by $$n$$ gives a biased but consistent estimator.

**Q3.** State the standard error of the sample mean.
> **A:** $$SE(\bar X) = \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \approx \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$$.

**Q4.** Define bias and efficiency of an estimator.
> **A:** Bias $$= E[\hat\theta] - \theta$$ (zero for unbiased). Efficiency: among unbiased estimators, the one with the smallest variance.

**Q5.** What does it mean that the sample mean is BLUE?
> **A:** Best Linear Unbiased Estimator — minimum variance among all estimators linear in the data and unbiased, needing only iid with finite variance.

**Q6.** Define consistency, and can a consistent estimator be biased?
> **A:** $$\hat\theta \to \theta$$ in probability as $$n \to \infty$$. Yes — it can be biased in finite samples (e.g., the $$n$$-divisor variance); the bias just vanishes asymptotically.

**Q7.** State the Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem.
> **A:** LLN: for iid data with finite variance, $$\bar X \to \mu$$. CLT: $$\dfrac{\bar X - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)$$ regardless of the underlying distribution.

**Q8.** What is the difference in what the LLN and CLT tell you?
> **A:** LLN says WHERE the sample mean goes (to $$\mu$$); CLT says HOW it fluctuates on the way (normally, with SD shrinking like $$1/\sqrt{n}$$).

**Q9.** Sample: 4, 6, 8, 10. Compute the unbiased sample variance.
> **A:** $$\bar X = 7$$; squared deviations 9, 1, 1, 9 sum to 20; $$s^2 = 20/3 = 6.67$$.

**Q10.** Quadrupling the sample size changes the standard error of the mean by what factor?
> **A:** $$SE \propto 1/\sqrt{n}$$, so $$1/\sqrt{4} = 1/2$$.

**Q11.** Give the sample covariance and correlation formulas.
> **A:** $$\hat\sigma_{XY} = \dfrac{1}{n-1}\sum (X_i - \bar X)(Y_i - \bar Y)$$; $$\hat\rho = \dfrac{\hat\sigma_{XY}}{s_X s_Y}$$.

**Q12.** What do coskewness and cokurtosis capture, and what are they for jointly normal variables?
> **A:** Cross co-moments for a pair; negative coskewness means one asset suffers when the other falls/is volatile. For jointly normal variables, coskewness is 0 and central cokurtosis is 1.

**Q13.** Losses have mean $10M, SD $30M, skewed. For $$n = 100$$, find $$P(\bar X > 16)$$ via the CLT.
> **A:** $$\bar X \approx N(10, 3^2)$$; $$P(Z > \tfrac{16-10}{3}) = P(Z > 2) = 2.28\%$$.

**Q14.** Why are sample skewness and kurtosis estimated imprecisely in small samples?
> **A:** They depend on rare extreme observations (third/fourth powers of deviations), so a few outliers dominate.
