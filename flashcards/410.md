**Q1.** Terminal value of $1 under m-times-per-year vs continuous compounding.
> **A:** m-compounding: $$\left(1+\frac{r}{m}\right)^{mT}$$. Continuous: $$e^{rT}$$.

**Q2.** Convert an m-compounded rate to a continuous rate and back.
> **A:** $$r_c = m\ln\!\left(1+\frac{r_m}{m}\right)$$ and $$r_m = m\left(e^{r_c/m}-1\right)$$.

**Q3.** Order the quoted rates that give the same wealth outcome.
> **A:** $$r_{annual} > r_{semi} > r_{monthly} > r_{cont}$$. More frequent compounding → lower quoted rate for the same effective return.

**Q4.** Continuous equivalent of 8% semiannual.
> **A:** $$r_c = 2\ln(1.04) = 2(0.039221) = 7.844\%$$. Effective annual = $$(1.04)^2-1 = 8.16\%$$.

**Q5.** Define the spot (zero) rate and relate it to the discount factor.
> **A:** z(t) is the rate on a zero maturing at t. Semiannual: $$d(t) = \frac{1}{(1+z(t)/2)^{2t}}$$. Continuous: $$d(t) = e^{-z(t)t}$$.

**Q6.** No-arbitrage forward rate (continuous compounding).
> **A:** $$f(t_1,t_2) = \frac{z_2 t_2 - z_1 t_1}{t_2 - t_1}$$ — the marginal rate for extending maturity.

**Q7.** Compute the year-2 forward: 1-year spot 3.00%, 2-year spot 3.50% (annual compounding).
> **A:** $$1+f = \frac{(1.035)^2}{1.03} = 1.040024 \Rightarrow f = 4.00\%$$ — above both spots, consistent with a rising curve.

**Q8.** Relationship between spot rates and forward rates.
> **A:** Spot rates are averages of forwards. Forwards above spots ⟺ rising spot curve; the forward curve amplifies the spot curve's slope. Forwards are break-even rates, not forecasts.

**Q9.** Par rate from discount factors (semiannual).
> **A:** $$c(T) = \frac{2(1 - d(T))}{A(T)}$$, where $$A(T) = \sum_i d(t_i)$$ is the annuity factor. The par rate makes the bond price exactly 100.

**Q10.** Compute c(1): d(0.5) = 0.9852, d(1.0) = 0.9690.
> **A:** $$c(1) = \frac{2(1-0.9690)}{0.9852+0.9690} = \frac{0.0620}{1.9542} = 3.17\%$$.

**Q11.** Order spot, forward, and par curves for each curve shape.
> **A:** Upward-sloping: forward > spot > par. Downward-sloping: forward < spot < par. Flat: all equal. (Par blends in earlier, lower spots via coupons.)

**Q12.** What is the most common semiannual-convention error?
> **A:** Failing to divide rates by 2 and double the periods — using $$(1+z)^t$$ on a semiannual-quoted rate. Also: plugging a semiannual rate into $$e^{rT}$$ overstates growth.

**Q13.** How can a borrower lock in a future borrowing rate, and what is maturity matching?
> **A:** Go long the $$t_1$$ zero and short the $$t_2$$ zero (or use an FRA/futures) to lock today's forward. Maturity matching = choosing asset maturities to match liability dates, removing rollover/reinvestment risk.
