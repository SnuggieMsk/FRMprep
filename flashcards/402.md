**Q1.** Name the three VaR estimation methods.
> **A:** Delta-normal (parametric), historical simulation, and Monte Carlo simulation.

**Q2.** Why does delta-normal VaR fail for option portfolios?
> **A:** It linearizes payoffs using delta and ignores gamma; option convexity produces skewed P&L even with normal underlying returns. Use full revaluation (historical sim or Monte Carlo).

**Q3.** Two-asset delta-normal portfolio standard deviation.
> **A:** $$\sigma_P = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 + 2\rho\,\sigma_1\sigma_2}$$, then $$\text{VaR}_\alpha = z_\alpha\sigma_P$$.

**Q4.** Compute delta-normal portfolio σ: $$\sigma_1=\$1.2M$$, $$\sigma_2=\$0.4M$$, ρ = 0.3.
> **A:** $$\sqrt{1.2^2+0.4^2+2(0.3)(1.2)(0.4)} = \sqrt{1.888} = \$1.374M$$.

**Q5.** How is VaR estimated under historical simulation?
> **A:** Revalue today's portfolio under the last n days' risk-factor changes, sort the n P&Ls, and take the $$n(1-\alpha)$$-th worst loss. ES = average of the worst $$n(1-\alpha)$$ losses.

**Q6.** From 100 simulated daily losses, find the 95% VaR.
> **A:** Rank = 100 × 0.05 = 5th worst loss. ES = average of the worst 5 losses.

**Q7.** Pros and cons of historical simulation.
> **A:** Pros: no distributional assumption, captures fat tails and non-linearity (full revaluation). Cons: limited by sample window, ghost effects as big days drop out, poor at extreme confidence with short windows.

**Q8.** Main weakness of each VaR method.
> **A:** Delta-normal: thin tails (normality). Historical sim: unrepresentative window. Monte Carlo: model misspecification ("garbage in, garbage out").

**Q9.** State the square-root-of-time rule and its assumptions.
> **A:** $$\text{VaR}_{T\text{-day}} = \text{VaR}_{1\text{-day}}\times\sqrt{T}$$. Assumes i.i.d. returns (no autocorrelation, constant vol) and zero drift.

**Q10.** Scale a $2M one-day 99% VaR to 25 days.
> **A:** $$2\times\sqrt{25}=2\times5=\$10M$$. (Multiplying by 25 instead of √25 is the classic trap.)

**Q11.** How does autocorrelation affect the square-root rule?
> **A:** Positive autocorrelation (trending) → √T understates true risk; mean reversion → overstates it. The rule applies to σ and VaR, not variance (which scales by T).

**Q12.** What is a backtesting "exception" and how many are expected?
> **A:** A day the realized loss exceeded the VaR forecast. Expected exceptions = $$n(1-\alpha)$$; at 99% over 250 days, expect 2.5. Count is binomial(n, 1−α).

**Q13.** Why is backtesting at 99% low power, and what else should be checked?
> **A:** Exceptions are rare, so it is hard to distinguish good from bad models quickly (95% gives more observations). Also check independence — clustered exceptions signal missed volatility dynamics.

**Q14.** Basel traffic-light backtesting thresholds (250 days).
> **A:** Green ≤ 4 exceptions; yellow 5–9 (capital multiplier rises); red ≥ 10.
