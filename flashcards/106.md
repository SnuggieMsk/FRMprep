**Q1.** What does a K-factor return-generating model look like?
> **A:** $$R_i = E[R_i] + \beta_{i1}F_1 + \cdots + \beta_{iK}F_K + e_i$$ where $$F_k$$ is the factor surprise (actual − expected, with $$E[F_k]=0$$), $$\beta_{ik}$$ the loading, and $$e_i$$ idiosyncratic return.

**Q2.** Exam trap: what part of a macro announcement moves returns in a factor model?
> **A:** Only the unanticipated component (surprise = actual − expected). If expected inflation is 2% and actual is 2%, the surprise is zero — no effect, however "high" 2% sounds.

**Q3.** What are the three APT assumptions?
> **A:** (1) Returns follow a factor model, (2) enough assets to diversify away idiosyncratic risk, (3) well-functioning markets allow no persistent arbitrage.

**Q4.** The APT pricing equation?
> **A:** $$E[R_i] = R_f + \beta_{i1}\lambda_1 + \cdots + \beta_{iK}\lambda_K$$ where $$\lambda_k$$ is the risk premium of factor $$k$$ (excess return of a pure factor portfolio with $$\beta_k=1$$, zero on all others).

**Q5.** APT vs. CAPM — logic and factors?
> **A:** CAPM rests on equilibrium/mean–variance optimization with one factor (the market). APT rests on no-arbitrage with multiple, unspecified factors and does not require the market portfolio or homogeneous expectations.

**Q6.** What does APT NOT require that CAPM does?
> **A:** APT does not require homogeneous expectations, that everyone holds the market portfolio, or an observable market portfolio. It only needs some arbitrageurs to enforce pricing in well-diversified portfolios.

**Q7.** What is the main weakness of APT?
> **A:** It is silent on what the factors actually are — its generality is also its weakness.

**Q8.** What is a pure factor portfolio, and why is it the hedging building block?
> **A:** A portfolio with beta 1 to one factor and 0 to all others. To hedge factor k, add positions so the net beta to that factor is zero while leaving desired exposures intact.

**Q9.** Name challenges of using multifactor models for hedging.
> **A:** Betas are estimated with error and drift over time; factor structure may be misspecified; hedging many factors needs many positions (costs, shorting limits); factor proxies are correlated.

**Q10.** The Fama–French three-factor model?
> **A:** $$E[R_i] - R_f = \beta_{i,m}(E[R_m]-R_f) + \beta_{i,SMB}E[SMB] + \beta_{i,HML}E[HML]$$.

**Q11.** What are SMB and HML?
> **A:** SMB ("small minus big") = small-cap minus large-cap return, the size premium. HML ("high minus low") = high book-to-market (value) minus low (growth), the value premium.

**Q12.** How do you read tilts from FF loadings?
> **A:** Positive SMB beta = small-cap tilt; positive HML beta = value tilt; negative HML beta = growth tilt. Watch the sign of negative loadings when summing premium terms.

**Q13.** What fourth factor is commonly added to Fama–French?
> **A:** A momentum factor (UMD/WML, the Carhart model).

**Q14.** Compute APT expected return: $$\beta_1=0.9$$ (premium 5%), $$\beta_2=0.2$$ (premium 1%), $$R_f=4\%$$.
> **A:** $$E[R] = 4\% + 0.9(5\%) + 0.2(1\%) = 4\% + 4.5\% + 0.2\% = 8.7\%$$.
