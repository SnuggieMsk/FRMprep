**Q1.** Name the main ingredients of the GFC build-up.
> **A:** Cheap money (1% Fed funds + global savings glut), a housing boom, collapsed underwriting (subprime/Alt-A, teaser ARMs, NINJA loans), securitization at scale via OTD, leverage everywhere (30–40x), and regulatory gaps (shadow banking, OTC derivatives).

**Q2.** Trace the subprime-to-CDO transmission machine.
> **A:** Brokers originate subprime for fees → loans pooled into RMBS, tranched into ~80% AAA → hard-to-sell mezzanine (BBB) tranches re-pooled into ABS CDOs (again rated AAA) → synthetic CDOs (CDS) multiply exposure → house prices fall, AAA tranches downgraded en masse.

**Q3.** Exam trap: does tranching destroy risk?
> **A:** No — it redistributes it. AAA ratings rested on low-correlation assumptions; a systematic shock (national house-price decline) hit all collateral at once, so "impossible" senior-tranche losses occurred. Re-securitization concentrated this exposure.

**Q4.** How did the issuer-pays model contribute to the crisis?
> **A:** Issuers shopped for and paid for ratings; agencies competing for structured-finance fees inflated grades — a conflict of interest that biased structured-product ratings upward, plus flawed correlation models.

**Q5.** What was the role of mortgage brokers/lenders?
> **A:** Originated loans for fees under originate-to-distribute with no skin in the game → fraudulent/weak underwriting (e.g., New Century, Countrywide).

**Q6.** How did commercial vs. investment banks fail?
> **A:** Commercial banks: off-balance-sheet conduits/SIVs and super-senior exposure came back on with too-thin capital (Citi, UBS). Investment banks: 30x+ leverage on overnight repo — Bear Stearns rescued (Mar 2008), Lehman failed (Sep 2008), Merrill sold.

**Q7.** What did AIG and the monolines do?
> **A:** Sold CDS protection on CDO tranches — a one-way bet. Downgrade-triggered collateral calls forced a $182bn AIG rescue.

**Q8.** What is the "run on repo"?
> **A:** As collateral values fell, repo lenders raised haircuts and refused to roll funding against securitized collateral, forcing deleveraging fire sales — the wholesale-market equivalent of a deposit run.

**Q9.** Why was short-term wholesale funding the systemic accelerant?
> **A:** It made lenders' positions runnable like deposits but without insurance; runs forced asset sales that depressed prices for everyone (fire-sale externalities), so funding liquidity and market liquidity collapsed together.

**Q10.** What happened with ABCP in August 2007?
> **A:** After BNP Paribas froze funds, investors refused to roll asset-backed commercial paper; the assets returned to the sponsor banks' balance sheets.

**Q11.** What direct money-market consequence followed Lehman's bankruptcy?
> **A:** Reserve Primary money fund held Lehman paper and "broke the buck," triggering a run on money market funds and a freeze in the commercial-paper market.

**Q12.** Key 2008 timeline anchors?
> **A:** Mar 2008: Bear Stearns sold to JPMorgan. Sep 2008: Fannie/Freddie conservatorship, Lehman bankruptcy (Sep 15), AIG rescue, Reserve Primary breaks the buck. Oct 2008: TARP ($700bn).

**Q13.** What central bank responses were deployed?
> **A:** Policy rates slashed to ~0–0.25%, broadened lender-of-last-resort facilities (TAF, PDCF, TSLF, CPFF, FX swap lines, money-fund backstops), and quantitative easing (Treasuries and agency MBS purchases).

**Q14.** Name the post-crisis reforms bridging to other chapters.
> **A:** Basel III (capital + LCR/NSFR), Dodd-Frank, Volcker rule, central clearing and margin for OTC derivatives, securitization risk retention, stress-testing regimes, and SIFI surcharges.
