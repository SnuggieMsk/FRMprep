**Q1.** How does machine learning differ from classical econometrics?
> **A:** Econometrics: theory-driven, small, interpretable, focused on unbiased estimates and hypothesis tests. ML: prioritizes out-of-sample predictive accuracy, tolerates biased/opaque models, handles huge feature sets and nonlinearities, validates empirically on held-out data.

**Q2.** Distinguish supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning.
> **A:** Supervised: labeled $$(X,Y)$$, predict $$Y$$ (regression/classification). Unsupervised: unlabeled $$X$$, find structure (PCA, clustering). Reinforcement: learn a policy of sequential actions from reward signals by trial and error.

**Q3.** Why standardize features, and which methods need it?
> **A:** Rescale via $$z = (x-\mu)/\sigma$$ so large-unit features don't dominate distance- and penalty-based methods (KNN, K-means, ridge/LASSO, SVM). Min-max scaling to $$[0,1]$$ is the alternative.

**Q4.** What are the roles of the training, validation, and test sets?
> **A:** Training: fit parameters. Validation: compare models / tune hyperparameters. Test: used ONCE at the end for an unbiased performance estimate.

**Q5.** Name two exam traps with data splits.
> **A:** (1) Tuning hyperparameters on the test set contaminates it (that's the validation set's job). (2) With time-series data, random shuffling leaks the future into training — splits must respect chronology.

**Q6.** Give the bias–variance decomposition of prediction error.
> **A:** $$E[(y-\hat f)^2] = \text{Bias}^2 + \text{Variance} + \sigma^2_{\text{noise}}$$ (too simple / too sensitive / irreducible).

**Q7.** Distinguish underfitting and overfitting and their signatures.
> **A:** Underfitting (high bias): too simple, poor fit in- AND out-of-sample. Overfitting (high variance): memorizes training noise — great in-sample, poor out-of-sample; signature is falling training error while validation error rises.

**Q8.** State the ridge and LASSO objectives and the key behavioral difference.
> **A:** Ridge (L2): $$\min \sum (y_i - X_i\beta)^2 + \lambda\sum\beta_j^2$$. LASSO (L1): $$\min \sum (y_i - X_i\beta)^2 + \lambda\sum\lvert\beta_j\rvert$$. Ridge shrinks all coefficients; LASSO can set some exactly to zero (feature selection).

**Q9.** What does the regularization parameter $$\lambda$$ do at its extremes, and what is it tuned on?
> **A:** $$\lambda = 0$$ gives OLS; $$\lambda \to \infty$$ shrinks everything to zero. It is tuned on validation data; regularization trades accepted bias for reduced variance.

**Q10.** What does PCA do, and which finance application is standard?
> **A:** Replaces correlated features with uncorrelated principal components — orthogonal linear combinations ordered by variance explained. Yield-curve moves compress to ~3 PCs: level, slope, curvature (95%+ of variance).

**Q11.** Describe the K-means algorithm.
> **A:** Choose $$K$$ and initialize centroids → assign each point to the nearest centroid (Euclidean) → recompute centroids as member means → repeat until assignments stop changing.

**Q12.** How do you choose $$K$$ and assess K-means quality?
> **A:** Inertia (within-cluster sum of squares) falls as $$K$$ rises — pick the "elbow," or maximize the silhouette score (cohesion vs. separation, range $$-1$$ to $$1$$). Results depend on initialization and scaling.

**Q13.** Centroids $$c_1 = (0,0)$$, $$c_2 = (4,2)$$. Assign point $$x = (3,0)$$.
> **A:** $$d_1 = 3.00$$, $$d_2 = \sqrt{(−1)^2 + (−2)^2} = \sqrt{5} = 2.24$$; $$d_2 < d_1$$ → assign to cluster 2.

**Q14.** Difference between ridge and LASSO under collinearity, and what is elastic net?
> **A:** Ridge handles collinearity well (shares weight among correlated regressors); LASSO picks one of a correlated group. Elastic net mixes both L1 and L2 penalties.
