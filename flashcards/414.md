**Q1.** The no-arbitrage replicating delta on a one-step tree.
> **A:** $$\Delta = \frac{f_u - f_d}{S_0 u - S_0 d}$$ — the number of shares making the stock-plus-short-option portfolio riskless. No probability assumption is needed, only no-arbitrage.

**Q2.** Risk-neutral up-probability.
> **A:** $$p = \frac{e^{r\Delta t} - d}{u - d}$$ — the up-probability in a fictitious world where every asset drifts at r. A pricing device, not a forecast.

**Q3.** Risk-neutral valuation formula on one step.
> **A:** $$f = e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p f_u + (1-p)f_d\right]$$ — expected payoff under p, discounted at the risk-free rate. Real-world probabilities and risk premia cancel out.

**Q4.** Calibrate u and d to volatility (CRR).
> **A:** $$u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}$$, $$d = 1/u = e^{-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}$$. With d = 1/u the tree recombines, so an n-step tree has n+1 terminal nodes.

**Q5.** Compute u with σ = 30% and monthly steps.
> **A:** $$u = e^{0.30\sqrt{1/12}} = e^{0.30(0.2887)} = e^{0.0866} = 1.0905$$. Forgetting √Δt is the trap.

**Q6.** Compute p: S = 50 → 60 or 45, r = 5% continuous, Δt = 1.
> **A:** u = 1.2, d = 0.9. $$p = \frac{e^{0.05} - 0.9}{1.2 - 0.9} = \frac{0.15127}{0.3} = 0.504$$.

**Q7.** How are European options valued on a two-step tree?
> **A:** Backward induction: value terminal payoffs, then roll back one step at a time with the one-step formula. Shortcut: discount probability-weighted terminal payoffs in one shot.

**Q8.** How are American options valued on a tree?
> **A:** Backward induction with one extra comparison at every non-terminal node: $$f_{node} = \max(\text{intrinsic value}, \text{continuation value})$$.

**Q9.** At a node an American put has intrinsic value 8.20, continuation 7.60. Node value?
> **A:** max(8.20, 7.60) = 8.20 — the holder exercises early.

**Q10.** The most common American-option valuation error.
> **A:** Discounting terminal payoffs directly (the European shortcut), which skips the node-by-node max(intrinsic, continuation) test and understates the American price.

**Q11.** Risk-neutral probability for each underlying type.
> **A:** Non-dividend stock: $$\frac{e^{r\Delta t}-d}{u-d}$$; index (yield q): $$\frac{e^{(r-q)\Delta t}-d}{u-d}$$; currency (foreign rate $$r_f$$): $$\frac{e^{(r-r_f)\Delta t}-d}{u-d}$$; futures: $$\frac{1-d}{u-d}$$.

**Q12.** Why is the futures growth factor 1, and what rate is used to discount?
> **A:** A futures position is costless to enter, so its risk-neutral drift is zero. Discounting is always at the domestic risk-free rate r, whatever sits in the numerator.

**Q13.** What happens as the number of tree steps grows?
> **A:** With u, d calibrated to σ, the binomial price of a European option converges to the Black-Scholes-Merton price (30+ steps is close). Trees remain the workhorse for American options, which BSM cannot price in closed form.
