# 11. GARP Code of Conduct

The Code binds every GARP member, FRM holder, and exam candidate. One or two near-free exam questions: know the principles, the professional standards, and the consequences of violation. Answer Code questions by the *strictest* reasonable reading.

## Learning objectives

- Describe the responsibility of each GARP member with respect to professional integrity, ethical conduct, conflicts of interest, confidentiality of information, and adherence to generally accepted practices in risk management.
- Describe the potential consequences of violating the GARP Code of Conduct.

## Who is bound, and how

The Code applies to **GARP members**, holders of GARP certifications (FRM, ERP/SCR), and **candidates registered for GARP exams**. By registering, you accept the Code. It applies to professional conduct generally — not only to acts performed in a formal "risk manager" role — and members must comply with the Code **even where local law or practice is laxer** (where law and the Code conflict, follow the stricter standard consistent with law).

## Principles

**1. Professional integrity and ethical conduct.** Members shall:
- Act professionally, ethically, and with integrity in all dealings.
- Exercise reasonable judgment and maintain independence of thought and direction; not offer, solicit, or accept gifts or benefits that could reasonably be expected to compromise (or appear to compromise) their own or others' independence.
- Take reasonable precautions to ensure their services are not used to deceive, manipulate markets, or violate law.
- Not knowingly misrepresent facts or make false or misleading statements (including about their own or others' qualifications and the meaning of the FRM designation).

**2. Conflicts of interest.** Members shall:
- Act fairly in all situations.
- **Fully disclose** any actual or potential conflict of interest to all affected parties — disclosure, not necessarily avoidance, is the operative duty.

**3. Confidentiality.** Members shall:
- Not make use of confidential information for **personal benefit** (their own or others' inappropriate advantage).
- Not reveal confidential information except where consent has been given or disclosure is **required by law** or by legal/regulatory process.

## Professional standards

**1. Fundamental responsibilities.** Members shall:
- Comply with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations (including GARP's own bylaws and policies) governing their professional activities.
- Have ethical responsibilities to the profession, to colleagues, to employers, and to the public, and not engage in conduct that compromises these responsibilities.
- Understand the needs and complexity of their employer or client and provide appropriate, competent services.
- Be diligent about not overstating the accuracy or certainty of results or conclusions.
- Maintain professional competence — keep current with risk management knowledge and best practices, and apply them.

**2. Adherence to generally accepted practices in risk management.** Members shall:
- Execute responsibilities with diligence, using generally accepted risk management practices.
- Ensure that communications of risk information are **clear, accurate, and not misleading** — including making assumptions, limitations, and the degree of uncertainty in models and analyses transparent.
- Make a distinction between fact and opinion when presenting analysis and recommendations.

{: .note }
Two themes the Code repeats: **transparency about limitations** (never overstate model accuracy or hide assumptions) and **disclosure** (conflicts must be fully disclosed to all affected parties). When two answer choices both look ethical, the one involving disclosure/escalation and honest communication of uncertainty is usually the Code-compliant one.

## Consequences of violation

- Violations may lead to formal investigation under GARP's disciplinary procedures.
- Sanctions can include **suspension or revocation of GARP membership** and of **the right to use the FRM (or other GARP) designation**; candidates can be barred from the exam program.
- Violations of *law* are additionally subject to referral to and action by legal, regulatory, or governmental authorities — the Code does not shield anyone from the law.
- Reputational damage: censure within the profession.

{: .warning }
Exam traps: (1) The Code does **not** say "avoid all conflicts of interest" — it requires acting fairly and **fully disclosing** actual or potential conflicts. (2) Confidentiality is not absolute — disclosure compelled **by law** (or with consent) is permitted. (3) The Code binds **candidates from registration**, not only certified FRMs. (4) "My country's rules allow it" is not a defense for conduct the Code prohibits.

## Application examples

- A risk manager presents a VaR figure to the board without mentioning that the model excludes the firm's largest illiquid position → violates *generally accepted practices* (clear, accurate, complete communication; disclosure of limitations).
- An FRM candidate lists herself as "FRM certified" while awaiting Part II results → misrepresentation of qualifications → violates *professional integrity*.
- A consultant recommends a software vendor without mentioning he receives referral fees → failure to disclose a **conflict of interest**.
- A member shares a client's portfolio details with a friend at a hedge fund → **confidentiality** violation (and likely law).
- A member receives a subpoena for client records and complies → **not** a violation; disclosure required by law is permitted.

## Quick quiz

**Q1.** A GARP member discovers her bonus depends partly on the trading desk whose risk she reports on. According to the Code, she must:
A. Resign immediately
B. Decline the bonus
C. Fully disclose the conflict to all affected parties
D. Keep silent unless asked

**Q2.** Under the Code, a member may reveal confidential client information when:
A. The information would benefit other clients
B. Disclosure is required by law
C. The client relationship has ended
D. The information is more than five years old

**Q3.** Which is a potential consequence of violating the GARP Code of Conduct?
A. Automatic criminal prosecution by GARP
B. Suspension or revocation of the right to use the FRM designation
C. A fine payable to GARP of up to $1 million
D. Mandatory retraining administered by the member's employer

**Q4.** A member presents a credit model's outputs to clients as "precise default probabilities" although the model has wide confidence bands. This most directly violates:
A. Confidentiality
B. Conflict-of-interest rules
C. The duty not to overstate accuracy and to communicate limitations clearly
D. Nothing — marketing language is exempt

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**Q1 — C.** The Code's remedy for conflicts is full disclosure to all affected parties.

**Q2 — B.** Exceptions: consent, or disclosure required by law/legal process.

**Q3 — B.** GARP can suspend/revoke membership and designation rights; it does not levy criminal penalties or fines.

**Q4 — C.** Members must not overstate accuracy or certainty and must make limitations and assumptions transparent.
