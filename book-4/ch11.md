# 11. Bond Yields and Return Calculations

Yield to maturity, realized returns, spreads, and the annuity/perpetuity toolkit. GARP tests price↔YTM mechanics, the premium/discount/par logic, decomposition of realized returns, and the reinvestment assumption buried inside YTM.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish gross vs net realized returns and compute holding-period returns with and without coupon reinvestment.
- Define and compute the spread of a bond over a benchmark curve.
- Define yield to maturity, compute price from YTM (and vice versa conceptually), and interpret YTM as a complex average of spot rates.
- Explain the price–coupon–yield relationships (premium/discount/par; pull to par).
- Use annuity and perpetuity formulas in bond calculations.
- Explain the reinvestment assumption in YTM and its consequences for realized returns.
- Decompose bond P&L into carry/coupon, rolldown, and rate-change components (in outline).

## Realized return

**Gross realized return** over a holding period:

$$R = \frac{P_{end} + \text{coupons received} + \text{reinvestment income} - P_{start}}{P_{start}}$$

**Net** return subtracts financing cost (e.g., repo interest if the position is leveraged). Multi-period returns must track when coupons arrive and at what rate they are reinvested — the realized return equals the YTM only if coupons are reinvested *at* the YTM and the bond is held to maturity.

### Worked example: realized return with reinvestment

Buy a 6% semiannual-coupon bond at 100.00; hold one year; sell at 100.50. The first coupon (3.00, received at 6 months) is reinvested for 6 months at 2% semiannually-compounded (i.e., 1% per half-year).

Coupon proceeds at year-end: $$3.00 \times 1.01 + 3.00 = 6.03$$

$$R = \frac{100.50 + 6.03 - 100}{100} = 6.53\% \text{ (annual, not annualized further)}$$

Lower reinvestment rates drag realized return below the purchase yield — visible even at a one-year horizon.

## Spread

The **spread** is the constant amount s added to every benchmark (e.g., Treasury) spot or forward rate so that discounted cash flows match the bond's market price:

$$P = \sum_t \frac{CF_t}{\left(1 + \frac{z_t + s}{2}\right)^{2t}}$$

It isolates the bond's value relative to the curve: compensation for credit risk, liquidity, and any mispricing. (A simpler version — yield spread — is just YTM minus the matched-maturity benchmark yield.)

## Yield to maturity

YTM (yield) y is the single discount rate equating PV of cash flows to price. Semiannual bond, T years, coupon c, face F:

$$P = \sum_{i=1}^{2T} \frac{cF/2}{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^{i}} + \frac{F}{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^{2T}}$$

Compactly, with the annuity factor:

$$P = \frac{cF}{y}\left[1 - \frac{1}{(1+\tfrac{y}{2})^{2T}}\right] + \frac{F}{(1+\tfrac{y}{2})^{2T}}$$

YTM is a cash-flow-weighted "complex average" of spot rates — it is bond-specific (two bonds with the same maturity but different coupons have different YTMs when the curve isn't flat).

**Properties:**

| Condition | Price |
|---|---|
| coupon rate > YTM | Premium (P > 100) |
| coupon rate = YTM | Par (P = 100) |
| coupon rate < YTM | Discount (P < 100) |

**Pull to par:** holding yield constant, a premium bond's price drifts *down* to 100 and a discount bond's *up* to 100 as maturity approaches.

### Worked example: price from YTM

2-year, 6% semiannual coupon, \$100 face, YTM 5%. Per-period rate 2.5%, 4 periods, coupon 3.

$$(1.025)^4 = 1.103813 \;\Rightarrow\; (1.025)^{-4} = 0.905951$$

Annuity factor: $$\frac{1 - 0.905951}{0.025} = 3.76197$$

$$P = 3 \times 3.76197 + 100 \times 0.905951 = 11.286 + 90.595 = \$101.88$$

Premium, as expected: coupon 6% > yield 5%. Calculator check (TI BA II+): N=4, I/Y=2.5, PMT=3, FV=100 → PV = −101.88.

### Worked example: perpetuity and annuity

A perpetuity pays \$4 per year; comparable yields are 5% (annual):

$$P = \frac{C}{y} = \frac{4}{0.05} = \$80$$

If yields fall to 4%, P = 100 — a 25% gain, illustrating the extreme duration of perpetuities. An annuity paying \$4 for 10 years at 5%: $$P = 4 \times \frac{1 - 1.05^{-10}}{0.05} = 4 \times 7.7217 = \$30.89$$.

## The reinvestment assumption

A bond's realized compound return equals its purchase YTM only if **every coupon is reinvested at the YTM** until maturity. If reinvestment rates are lower → realized < YTM; higher → realized > YTM.

- Short horizon: dominated by **price risk** (rates up → loss).
- Long horizon: dominated by **reinvestment risk** (rates up → coupons compound faster → gain).
- At the horizon equal to the bond's **duration**, the two roughly offset — the basis of immunization.

{: .warning }
YTM is **not** a promised realized return. It is a quoting convention. Zero-coupon bonds are the exception: no coupons → no reinvestment assumption → YTM held to maturity *is* the realized return. Also: a semiannual YTM of 5% means 2.5% per period — annualizing by compounding gives an effective annual 5.06%; mixing bond-equivalent and effective yields is a recurring distractor.

{: .tip }
P&L decomposition intuition: return = coupon income (carry) + rolldown (price change from sliding along an unchanged curve) + impact of curve changes + spread change. Questions usually ask which component remains if "the curve is unchanged" — carry + rolldown.

## Quick quiz

**1.** A 3-year, 4% annual-coupon bond yields 5%. Its price is closest to:
- A. \$97.28
- B. \$100.00
- C. \$102.78
- D. \$96.62

**2.** A bond trades at a discount. Then:
- A. Coupon rate > YTM
- B. Coupon rate < YTM
- C. Coupon rate = YTM
- D. The bond must be callable

**3.** An investor's realized compound return on a coupon bond held to maturity will exceed the purchase YTM if:
- A. Coupons are reinvested below the YTM
- B. Coupons are reinvested above the YTM
- C. The bond was bought at par
- D. Never — YTM is the maximum

**4.** A perpetuity paying \$6 annually when yields are 8% is worth:
- A. \$48
- B. \$75
- C. \$133
- D. \$60

**2. B.** Price below par ⟺ coupon below yield.

**3. B.** Higher reinvestment rates push realized return above the quoted YTM.
