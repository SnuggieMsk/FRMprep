# 7. Operational Risk

GARP tests the Basel definition and event taxonomy, the frequency/severity modeling split, the evolution of capital approaches culminating in the Standardized Measurement Approach (SMA), and the management toolkit (RCSA, KRIs, scenario analysis).

## Learning objectives

- Define operational risk (Basel) and describe the seven event-type categories, including cyber, compliance, and rogue-trader risk.
- Explain how op-risk losses are modeled via frequency and severity distributions (loss distribution approach).
- Describe the Basel capital approaches: Basic Indicator, Standardized, AMA, and the current SMA (business indicator and internal loss multiplier).
- Explain the use of scenario analysis, external data, and business environment factors, especially for tail events.
- Describe risk and control self-assessment (RCSA), key risk indicators (KRIs), and loss data collection.
- Explain moral hazard/adverse incentives and the allocation of op-risk capital to business lines.

## Definition and taxonomy

Basel: *the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems, or from external events*. **Includes legal risk; excludes strategic and reputational risk.**

Seven Basel event types:

| Event type | Example |
|---|---|
| 1. Internal fraud | **Rogue trader** (Barings/Leeson, SocGén/Kerviel), embezzlement |
| 2. External fraud | **Cyber attacks**, theft, forgery |
| 3. Employment practices & workplace safety | Discrimination claims |
| 4. Clients, products & business practices | **Compliance failures**: mis-selling, money-laundering fines, market manipulation (largest dollar losses historically) |
| 5. Damage to physical assets | Natural disasters, terrorism |
| 6. Business disruption & system failures | IT outages |
| 7. Execution, delivery & process management | Settlement errors, data entry (highest frequency, low severity) |

- **High-frequency/low-severity (HFLS)** losses (processing errors) are budgetable, near EL.
- **Low-frequency/high-severity (LFHS)** losses (rogue trader, massive fines) drive the capital tail.
- **Cyber risk** spans external fraud and business disruption; growing, fat-tailed, and hard to model with internal data alone. **Compliance/conduct risk** sits in type 4 and produced the biggest post-2008 losses (multi-billion fines).

## Modeling: frequency × severity

The loss distribution approach (LDA) builds annual aggregate loss as a compound distribution:

- **Frequency** (number of losses per year): typically **Poisson** with intensity λ.
- **Severity** (size of each loss): fat-tailed, typically **lognormal** (or extreme-value distributions for the tail).

Combine by Monte Carlo: draw N ~ Poisson(λ), draw N severities, sum; repeat to get the aggregate annual loss distribution. Capital = 99.9% quantile − expected loss (or the full quantile, per regulation).

$$E[\text{annual loss}] = \lambda \times E[\text{severity}]$$

### Worked example: expected aggregate loss

A business line suffers operational losses with Poisson frequency λ = 12 per year; severity is lognormal with mean \$80,000.

$$E[\text{loss}] = 12 \times 80{,}000 = \$960{,}000 \text{ per year}$$

Note what this *doesn't* tell you: the 99.9% tail depends on severity's tail (the lognormal σ), which dominates capital. Two units with identical EL can need very different capital.

{: .note }
Data problems: internal loss data is short and truncated at reporting thresholds; **external data** (consortium, e.g., ORX) needs scaling for firm size; **scenario analysis** fills the tail where data are absent; **BEICFs** (business environment and internal control factors) adjust for current control quality. These were the four AMA inputs.

## Basel capital approaches

| Approach | Capital basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic Indicator (BIA)** | 15% of average annual gross income | Legacy |
| **Standardized (TSA)** | 12–18% (β) of gross income by business line | Legacy |
| **AMA** | Internal model (LDA + scenarios), 99.9%/1yr | Withdrawn — too much variance across banks, gaming |
| **SMA** (current, Basel III final) | Formula: see below | Standard since 2023 |

**SMA in outline:**

$$\text{Capital} = BIC \times ILM$$

- **Business Indicator (BI):** a size/income proxy (interest, services, and financial components).
- **BIC:** BI × marginal coefficients (~12%/15%/18% across three size buckets) — bigger banks, higher marginal rate.
- **Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM):** scales capital up (down) if the bank's 10-year average historical losses are high (low) relative to BIC:

$$ILM = \ln\!\left(e - 1 + \left(\frac{LC}{BIC}\right)^{0.8}\right), \quad LC = 15 \times \text{average annual losses}$$

ILM = 1 when LC = BIC; national discretion may set ILM = 1. Know the *structure* (size-based charge scaled by loss experience), not coefficient details.

### Worked example: SMA direction

Bank A and Bank B have the same BIC of \$2.0B. Bank A's loss component LC = BIC (average annual op losses in line with peers) → ILM = ln(e − 1 + 1) = ln(e) = 1, capital = \$2.0B. Bank B's LC = 2 × BIC → ILM = ln(e − 1 + 2^{0.8}) = ln(1.718 + 1.741) = ln(3.459) = 1.241 → capital = \$2.48B. Worse loss history → ~24% more capital.

## Management toolkit

- **RCSA (risk and control self-assessment):** business units periodically identify risks, rate likelihood/impact, and assess control effectiveness. Strength: ownership and coverage of un-materialized risks. Weakness: subjectivity, self-serving bias.
- **KRIs (key risk indicators):** forward-looking metrics tied to risk drivers — staff turnover, system downtime, unreconciled trades, failed settlements, audit findings — with thresholds triggering escalation.
- **Loss data collection:** comprehensive internal event database (amount, type, business line, near-misses).
- **Scenario analysis:** structured expert workshops quantifying plausible LFHS events ("what would a 1-in-40-year cyber breach cost?") — essential where data is sparse; vulnerable to anchoring and overconfidence biases.
- **Mitigation:** controls, segregation of duties, limits, business-continuity planning, and **insurance** (transfers severity; beware basis risk, payout delays, counterparty risk; Basel caps insurance offsets).

{: .warning }
Trap: operational risk is **not** reduced by holding capital — capital *absorbs* losses; controls *reduce* them. Also, allocating op-risk capital to business lines by loss history alone creates **moral hazard** (units hide losses) — allocation should reward good control environments (lower capital → incentive to improve controls). And remember the definition boundary: strategic and reputational risks are *excluded* from Basel op risk; legal risk is *included*.

{: .tip }
Rogue-trader pattern recognition: unsupervised star trader, no segregation between front and back office, unexplained profits, never takes holidays. "Profitable rule-breaking punished leniently" breeds the next disaster — discipline must be outcome-independent.
